% Stage D: the set-up of a search: the leading zeros and the degree, the refusal of a binary form
% that is not squarefree with the remainder chain of sturm.c, the reversal with its three cases, the
% coefficients as longs, the exit when the curve has no points modulo a prime, and the points at
% infinity in all cases.
\chapter{The set-up}\label{chap:setup}

Stage~D models what ratpoints does with its input before the sieve: it drops the leading zero
coefficients, refuses a binary form that is not squarefree, reverses the polynomial in three cases,
reads the coefficients as \verb|long|s where they fit, ends the search when the curve has no points
modulo one of the primes it examines, and delivers the points at infinity, after a reversal as
affine points of the caller's curve. With it the refusal theorem of Section~\ref{sec:refusals} has
its proof: with the Sturm step on, as in a run of the program without options, $\mathrm{findPoints}$
refuses $(c, H)$ exactly when $c$ has fewer than two or more than $\mathrm{maxDegree} + 1$ entries,
$H = 0$ or $H \ge 2^{63}$, or $\Phi_c$ is not squarefree; with the Sturm step off, it refuses
exactly what the program refuses under \verb|-s|; and since stage~H both add the search intervals of
the options that are not well formed, the refusal of the reversal check being met only for the whole
box (Chapter~\ref{chap:region}).

The reversal is the transformation of Chapter~\ref{chap:sieve}: it exchanges $x$ and $z$ in the
binary form, and the map that the C code applies to every point it finds of the reversed polynomial
is a bijection onto the points of the caller's curve that preserves the box. So after a reversal the
output is that of the search of the reversed polynomial with each point mapped back, a permutation
of the reference; the reduction of the run to the reference (Theorem~\ref{thm:run_reference}) holds
for any polynomial and its degree, membership holds with $\mathrm{noCheck}$ false, the set of
elements with both options of the output off, and no duplicates for every option, since stage~H
membership, the set of elements and the order for the points in the search region of the options.
The set-up has been the most error-prone part of ratpoints: the fixes of 2.2.2 include a reversal
that marked a cubic reversed and left it as it was, and those of 2.2.4 a search that returned before
the points at infinity when the Sturm step found no real point in the search region.

$\mathrm{findPoints}$ takes an argument for the options of the library that change the refusals or
the order of the output, and the program reads the options that set them: \verb|-k| and \verb|-K|,
which forbid and allow the reversal, and \verb|-s| and \verb|-S|, which switch the Sturm step off
and on; since stage~H they hold the search region too, the search intervals and the range of the
denominators (Chapter~\ref{chap:region}). Of the Sturm step, stage~D models the test for a repeated
factor, its first half; stage~H models the rest, which restricts the numerators to the intervals
where $f$ may be $\ge 0$ (Section~\ref{sec:sturmStep}), and proves, assuming Sturm's theorem, that
with the exact check on the depth of the step changes only the speed
(Theorem~\ref{thm:sturmSpeed}).

\section{What the C code does before the sieve}\label{sec:setupC}

For the coefficient list $c_0, \dots, c_n$ and the height $H$, the program checks the number of
coefficients and the height before it calls the library (\csource{main.c}{362}{380}), and
\verb|find_points_work| refuses a degree outside $0, \dots, \mathrm{maxDegree}$
(\csource{find_points.c}{4557}{4558}). Then \verb|find_points_work_1|:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item drops the leading zero coefficients and refuses the form as not squarefree
    (\verb|RATPOINTS_NON_SQUAREFREE|) when that lowers $\lceil n/2 \rceil$
    (\csource{find_points.c}{4624}{4633}): for the degree $d$ that is left and $N = 2 \lceil n/2
    \rceil$ this is $d + 2 \le N$, that is $Z^2 \mid \Phi_c$; it covers the zero polynomial and
    every list whose only nonzero entry is $c_0$, so that the refusal of degree $0$ which follows is
    never met from the program; it records whether $c_d$ is a square
    (\csource{find_points.c}{4634}{4634}), caps the parameters and refuses $H < 1$, which the
    program never passes;
  \item unless the caller forbids it (\verb|RATPOINTS_NO_REVERSE|, the program's \verb|-k|) or gives
    search intervals or bounds for the denominators (\csource{find_points.c}{4686}{4687}, stage~H,
    Chapter~\ref{chap:region}), checks whether to reverse the polynomial
    (\csource{find_points.c}{4732}{4803}): in case~1, $d$ even and $c_0 = 0$, it refuses the form as
    not squarefree if also $c_1 = 0$, since then $X^2 \mid \Phi_c$, and otherwise reverses
    $c_0, \dots, c_d$ and lowers the degree to $d - 1$; in case~2, $d$ even, $c_d$ a square and
    $c_0$ not, it reverses $c_0, \dots, c_d$; in case~3, $d$ odd, $|c_d| \ne 1$, $c_0 = 0$ and
    $|c_1| = 1$, it reverses $c_1, \dots, c_d$; in each case the binary form $F'$ of the new list
    has the degree $D$ of $F$ and $F'(x, z) = F(z, x)$, and the new leading coefficient is not zero;
  \item records whether the coefficients fit into \verb|long|s
    (\csource{find_points.c}{4814}{4823}), so that \verb|examine_prime| reduces them modulo $p$ with
    the helper \verb|mod| (\csource{find_points.c}{477}{492}) rather than with GMP;
  \item with the Sturm step on (\verb|args->sturm| $\ge 0$, by default $10$; the program's \verb|-s|
    sets $-1$, and \verb|-S| the default or a depth), computes the remainder chain of $f$ and $f'$
    (\csource{sturm.c}{99}{163}) and refuses the form as not squarefree when a remainder is zero
    (\csource{find_points.c}{4857}{4863}, \csource{find_points.c}{4879}{4879}); the rest of the step
    is stage~H (Section~\ref{sec:sturmStep}), and so is its exit when $f$ is negative on the whole
    search region, which changes no output, since the search region holds no affine point then and
    the exit comes only for an even degree whose leading coefficient is not a square, without points
    at infinity, and the return after the points at infinity when the step leaves nothing to search
    (\csource{find_points.c}{5106}{5106});
  \item decides whether there are points at infinity (\csource{find_points.c}{4894}{4899}) and sets
    up the tests on the denominators and the 2-adic information, with an exit of its own that
    changes no output either (stage~E);
  \item examines the primes, and the first prime modulo which the curve has no points, neither a
    residue $x$ with $f(x)$ a square nor a point at infinity, ends the search with no points
    (\csource{find_points.c}{1761}{1762}, \csource{find_points.c}{2605}{2614},
    \csource{find_points.c}{2994}{2998}, \csource{find_points.c}{4992}{5012}); the primes it
    examines are the first \verb|num_primes| of the table, and more when the number was left to the
    library;
  \item delivers the points at infinity, as $(0, y, 1)$ after a reversal
    (\csource{find_points.c}{5068}{5105}), and runs the sieve, whose check of a survivor $(a, b)$ of
    the reversed polynomial delivers $(b, y, a)$ for $a \ge 0$ and $(-b, y, -a)$ for $a < 0$
    (\csource{find_points.c}{5250}{5338}).
\end{enumerate}
The program turns \verb|RATPOINTS_NON_SQUAREFREE| into its error~8, the message
\verb|Polynomial is not square-free.| and the exit status~8 (\csource{main.c}{256}{261},
\csource{main.c}{1047}{1047}). No point is printed before it, since all three refusals come before
the first point: with \verb|-q| nothing at all, and without \verb|-q| the banner and the curve
(\csource{main.c}{215}{220}), then the height bound, or under \verb|-v|, for the refusals of steps~2
and~4, the report of the library in its place (for that of step~1 nothing, since it comes before the
report). With the Sturm step on, the C code refuses the form exactly when $\Phi_c$ is not
squarefree: $Z^2 \mid \Phi_c$ in step~1, $X^2 \mid \Phi_c$ for even $d$ in step~2, and a repeated
factor of the polynomial searched in step~4; with the Sturm step off only steps~1 and~2 remain, and
step~2 only when the reversal check runs. After a reversal the program prints the points of the
reversed polynomial in the order of its search, each mapped back; where the denominators ascend,
this is the list of the brute-force reference for the reversed polynomial, mapped. The exit of
step~6 never fires after a reversal (every case leaves the caller's curve a point of height~$1$),
and when it fires the curve has no points at all, so the primes it examines cannot change the
output.

  \uses{def:Double}
  \lean{Ratpoints.Options, Ratpoints.defaultSturm}
  \leanok
  The options of the library that change the refusals, the order of the output or what is delivered,
  an argument of $\mathrm{findPoints}$ whose defaults are those of a run of the program without
  options: whether the set-up may reverse the polynomial (the bit \verb|RATPOINTS_NO_REVERSE| of the
  flags clear), \verb|args->sturm|, the depth of the Sturm step, negative for none, with the default
  $\mathrm{defaultSturm} = 10$ (\verb|RATPOINTS_DEFAULT_STURM|, \csource{ratpoints.h}{341}{343}; the
  program's defaults \csource{main.c}{348}{348}, \csource{main.c}{352}{352}), and the options of the
  output $\mathrm{noCheck}$ and $\mathrm{noY}$, false by default, the bits \verb|RATPOINTS_NO_CHECK|
  and \verb|RATPOINTS_NO_Y| of the flags (\csource{ratpoints.h}{403}{405}, with
  \verb|RATPOINTS_NO_REVERSE|), and the search region: the search intervals, a list of pairs of
  doubles (Definition~\ref{def:Double}), empty by default, for the array \verb|domain| of the type
  \verb|ratpoints_interval| with its number \verb|num_inter|, and the least and the largest
  denominator, integers like the \verb|long|s \verb|b_low| and \verb|b_high|, with the defaults $1$
  and $0$, the latter meaning $H$ (\csource{ratpoints.h}{359}{359}, \csource{ratpoints.h}{363}{364},

  parameters, of the choice of the moduli and of the options of the program, which the deviation
  names). The depth switches the test for a repeated factor on when it is not negative, and the
  Sturm step of the search (Definition~\ref{def:sturmRegion}) reads it, capped at $55$
  (Definition~\ref{def:normalize}); stage~G added the options of the output, which change what is
  delivered, as the reversal does too under $\mathrm{noCheck}$, and stage~H the search region
  (Chapter~\ref{chap:region}).
\end{definition}

The options are not parameters: the parameters are the free choices that change neither the points
nor their order (Definition~\ref{def:Params}), while the reversal changes the order and, without the
Sturm step, the refusals, and the search region what is delivered, the order (by the largest
denominator, which decides the shape of the divisors, and by the reversal, which it forbids) and the
refusals. $\mathrm{defaultSturm}$, an integer like the C \verb|long| that it stands for, which a
build may make negative, is one of the constants of \verb|Ratpoints/Model/Constants.lean|, and no
proof depends on its value or its sign. In ratpoints~3.1.1 the depth of the Sturm step changes only
the speed: the intervals that it keeps hold every point of the box in the search intervals of the
caller, which stage~H proves assuming Sturm's theorem, so that with the exact check on the search
with the step off gives the same list (Theorem~\ref{thm:sturmSpeed}).

\section{The squarefree binary form}\label{sec:squarefree}

The refusals speak about $\Phi_c$, the C code about $f = \sum_i c_i X^i$, its degree $d$ and its
reversal. Chapter~\ref{chap:spec} names the facts that connect them; Mathlib has none of them, and
they are proved at Mathlib's generality, the general ones in \verb|Ratpoints/Mathlib| as candidates
for Mathlib.

\begin{lemma}[Homogenizations and squarefreeness]\label{lem:homogenize}
  \lean{MvPolynomial.IsHomogeneous.of_mul_left, Polynomial.homogenize_eq_homogenize_natDegree_mul, Polynomial.not_X_one_dvd_homogenize_natDegree, Polynomial.squarefree_of_squarefree_homogenize, Polynomial.squarefree_homogenize_iff, Polynomial.homogenize_reflect, MulEquiv.squarefree_iff, Polynomial.squarefree_homogenize_reflect_iff}
  \leanok
  Over a commutative semiring without zero divisors, a factor of a nonzero homogeneous polynomial
  is homogeneous. For a polynomial $p$ over a domain and $m \ge \deg p$, the homogenization of $p$
  of degree $m$ is squarefree if and only if $p$ is squarefree and $m \le \deg p + 1$. Over a
  commutative semiring, the homogenization of degree $m$ of the reflection of $p$ in degree $m$ is
  that of $p$ with the two variables exchanged, for every $p$ and $m$; squarefreeness is preserved
  by multiplicative equivalences; so the reflection does not change whether the homogenization is
  squarefree.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  In a product over a semiring without zero divisors the lowest degrees add up (the order of the
  power series) and so do the highest (the total degree), so a factor of a homogeneous polynomial
  has the two equal. If $m \ge \deg p + 2$, the square of $Z$ divides the homogenization, and if
  $p = a^2 q$, the homogenizations of $a$ and $q$ give a square factor. Conversely, a factor $A$ of
  the homogenization with $A^2$ dividing it is homogeneous; $Z$ does not divide it, since
  $m \le \deg p + 1$ and the homogenization of degree $\deg p$ takes the leading coefficient of $p$
  at $(1, 0)$; and $A$ with $Z = 1$, squared, divides $p$, so it is a unit, a constant, and so is
  $A$. The reflection compares the coefficients of the two homogenizations, which agree for every
  $p$ and $m$, and exchanging the variables is a ring automorphism.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The refusal predicate in the terms of the C code]\label{lem:squarefree_binaryFormPoly}
  \uses{def:binaryFormPoly, lem:homogenize}
  \lean{Ratpoints.poly, Ratpoints.getD_degree_ne_zero_of_pos, Ratpoints.natDegree_poly, Ratpoints.binaryFormPoly_eq_homogenize, Ratpoints.squarefree_binaryFormPoly_iff, Ratpoints.not_squarefree_binaryFormPoly_of_lt, Ratpoints.not_squarefree_binaryFormPoly_of_getD_zero}
  \leanok
  For $d \ge 1$ the entry $c_d$ is not zero; the polynomial $f = \sum_i c_i X^i \in \QQ[X]$ has the
  degree $d$ of $c$, and $\Phi_c$ is its homogenization of degree $N$; so $\Phi_c$ is squarefree if
  and only if $N \le d + 1$ and $f$ is squarefree. In particular $d + 2 \le N$ gives a form that is
  not squarefree, and so does $c_0 = c_1 = 0$, for every degree.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The entries above $d$ are $0$, and $c_d \ne 0$ for $d \ge 1$, so $f$ has degree $d$, and $\Phi_c$
  is the homogenization of $f$ of degree $N \ge n \ge d$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:homogenize} gives the
  equivalence; $d + 2 \le N$ violates $N \le d + 1$; and $c_0 = c_1 = 0$ gives $X^2 \mid f$, where
  $X$ is not a unit, so that $f$ is not squarefree, $f = 0$ included.
\end{proof}

On every input with $N \le d + 1$, $D = N$, and $\Phi_c$ evaluates to $F_c$
(Definition~\ref{def:binaryFormPoly}); so the predicate of the refusals and the form of the points
fit together on every input that passes the test of the leading zeros, in particular on every input
that is not refused.

\section{The remainder chain}\label{sec:chain}

\begin{definition}[The remainder chain]\label{def:sturmChain}
  \lean{Ratpoints.Sturm.reduce, Ratpoints.Sturm.chain, Ratpoints.Sturm.fullReduce, Ratpoints.Sturm.normalize, Ratpoints.Sturm.IsChain, Ratpoints.Sturm.IsChainResult, Ratpoints.Sturm.chain_spec, Ratpoints.Sturm.IsChain.size_le, Ratpoints.Sturm.IsChain.size_getElem!_add_one_add_le}
  \leanok
  For the coefficients $c_0, \dots, c_d$ of $f$, $d \ge 1$ and $c_d \ne 0$: $s_0 = f$, $s_1 = f'$
  (the coefficients $(i + 1) c_{i+1}$), and for $k = 2, \dots, d$ the remainder $t$ of $s_{k-2}$ by
  $s_{k-1}$ as \verb|sturm.c| computes it in integers: starting from $t = s_{k-2}$, while $\deg t
  \ge \deg s_{k-1}$, $t$ becomes $u t - v X^{\deg t - \deg s_{k-1}} s_{k-1}$, where $u$ and $v$ are
  the leading coefficients of $s_{k-1}$ and $t$ divided by their positive $\gcd$ and signed so that
  $u > 0$, and its zero leading coefficients are dropped; the chain ends with $\mathrm{none}$ if $t$
  becomes zero, and otherwise $s_k$ is $-t$ divided by the $\gcd$ of its coefficients, the chain
  ending when $s_k$ is constant (\csource{sturm.c}{99}{163}). For $d = 1$ the loop over $k$ does not
  run, and the chain is $f, f'$, with $f' = c_1$ constant; for $d \ge 2$ it ends at its \verb|break|
  at a constant $s_k$ or at the return of $\mathrm{none}$, never at its bound, since $\deg s_k \le d
  - k$. The loops are those of the C code: the loop over $k$, the reduction while the degree of $t$
  is at least that of $s_{k-1}$, the drop of the zero coefficients, and the loop for the $\gcd$ of
  the coefficients, which stops when the $\gcd$ is $1$. Each $s_k$ is the array of its coefficients
  up to its degree, which stands for a row of the C code's table of $(d + 1)^2$ GMP integers
  together with its entry in the array of degrees; one reduction step, $\mathrm{reduce}$, a helper
  inside the lines of the chain, has no C range of its own, as \verb|Sieve.evalMod| has none inside
  those of the tables of a prime power (Definition~\ref{def:examinePower}). The contract, with Lean
  core's program logic: the loops end by their measures, the sizes of the remainders and of the
  arrays whose zeros are dropped; and the result is a chain in the sense of the named predicate
  $\mathrm{IsChain}$ (its first two elements are $f$ and $f'$, each further one is the full
  reduction ($\mathrm{fullReduce}$) of the element two before by the element before, negated and
  divided by the $\gcd$ of its coefficients ($\mathrm{normalize}$), every element has a nonzero
  leading coefficient, and the sizes decrease from $s_1$ on) whose last element is constant, or
  $\mathrm{none}$, and then a chain in this sense exists whose last element has positive degree and
  reduces the element before it to zero. A chain has at most $d + 1$ rows, and its row $k + 1$ at
  most $d - k$ entries, the bounds of the table of the C code.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The chain detects a repeated factor]\label{lem:sturmChain_squarefree}
  \uses{def:sturmChain, lem:squarefree_binaryFormPoly}
  \lean{Ratpoints.Sturm.IsChain.exists_mul_eq, Ratpoints.Sturm.IsChain.natDegree_lt, Ratpoints.Sturm.run_chain_eq_none_iff, isCoprime_iff_of_mul_eq_mul_sub_mul}
  \leanok
  For $d \ge 1$ and $c_d \ne 0$, the chain is $\mathrm{none}$ if and only if $f$ is not squarefree
  over $\QQ$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The polynomial of an array of coefficients is $\mathrm{poly}$ of its list
  (Lemma~\ref{lem:squarefree_binaryFormPoly}). Each $s_k$, $k \ge 2$, satisfies $A s_{k-2} = Q
  s_{k-1} - g s_k$ with integers $A \ge 1$ and $g \ge 1$ and some $Q \in \QQ[X]$, read off the
  arrays coefficient by coefficient; for units $a$ and $b$ of a commutative ring, $a x = q y - b z$
  makes $y$ and $z$ coprime exactly when $x$ and $y$ are, so coprimality passes along the chain. A
  zero remainder after $s_{k-1}$ of degree at least $1$ makes $s_{k-1}$ a common factor of positive
  degree, and a nonzero constant makes the pair coprime. Over $\QQ$, $f$ and $f'$ are coprime
  exactly when $f$ is separable, which in characteristic $0$ is squarefreeness. The converse of the
  contract's clause for $\mathrm{none}$ follows from this lemma: such a chain makes its element of
  positive degree a common factor of $f$ and $f'$, so that $f$ is not squarefree, and the chain is
  $\mathrm{none}$.
\end{proof}

The relation with positive $A$ and $g$ and the decreasing degrees is also what stage~H reads off the
chain: its rows are positive multiples of the Sturm sequence of $f$ and $f'$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:chainSturm}), whose signs Sturm's theorem reads, and the Sturm step counts their
sign changes; so the chain is computed as the C code computes it, row by row, and not only its
verdict.

\section{The reversal}\label{sec:reversal}

\begin{definition}[The reversal check]\label{def:reversal}
  \lean{Ratpoints.Setup.isSquare, Ratpoints.Setup.reversal, Ratpoints.Refusal, Ratpoints.Refusal.notSquarefree}
  \leanok
  For the switch of the reversal and the coefficients $c$ of degree $d$, the leading zeros dropped:
  if the reversal is forbidden, $c$ itself; otherwise, for even $d$ with $c_0 = 0$, the refusal if
  $c_1 = 0$, and otherwise the list $c_d, \dots, c_1, c_0$ with the degree $d - 1$ (case~1); for
  even $d$ with $c_d$ a square and $c_0$ not, the list $c_d, \dots, c_0$ (case~2); for odd $d$ with
  $|c_d| \ne 1$, $c_0 = 0$ and $|c_1| = 1$, the list $c_0, c_d, \dots, c_1$ (case~3); and otherwise
  $c$ itself; with a flag that says whether the list is reversed
  (\csource{find_points.c}{4732}{4803}). The squares are those of \verb|mpz_perfect_square_p|: $0$
  and the squares of the positive integers. The switch is the test of the flags that guards the C
  block, the two bits of which, the caller's \verb|RATPOINTS_NO_REVERSE| and
  \verb|RATPOINTS_NO_REVERSE_AUTO|, which a search region or a range of the denominators sets, the
  search folds into one, the switch of the normalized options (Definition~\ref{def:normalize}). The
  refusals of a search carry the errors of the program: more than $\mathrm{maxDegree} + 1$
  coefficients (3), fewer than two (5), the height bound $0$ or one that is not a C \verb|long| (4),
  a binary form that is not squarefree (8), and search intervals that are not well formed (9,
  Definition~\ref{def:searchIntervals}); the check returns the binary form that is not squarefree
  for even $d$ with $c_0 = c_1 = 0$, as the test of the leading zeros and the remainder chain do for
  the forms they find.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The reversal exchanges $x$ and $z$]\label{lem:reversal_swap}
  \uses{def:reversal, def:binaryForm}
  \lean{Ratpoints.Setup.getD_eq_of_reversal_eq_ok, Ratpoints.Setup.degree_eq_of_reversal_eq_ok, Ratpoints.Setup.eq_of_reversal_eq_ok_false, Ratpoints.Setup.binaryForm_eq_of_reversal_eq_ok, Ratpoints.binaryForm_eq_of_getD_eq_getD_sub, Ratpoints.Setup.one_le_of_reversal_eq_ok, Ratpoints.Setup.exists_reversal_eq_error_iff}
  \leanok
  The check refuses exactly when its switch is on, $d$ is even and $c_0 = c_1 = 0$. For $d$ the
  degree of $c$: if the check returns the list $c'$ with the degree $d'$, then $d'$ is the degree of
  $c'$, $d' \ge 1$ when $d \ge 1$, since the check lowers only an even $d \ge 2$, to $d - 1$, and
  without a reversal $c' = c$ and $d' = d$; if it returns them as reversed, then $c'$ is the
  reflection of $c$ in degree $D$, $c'_i = c_{D-i}$ for $i \le D$ and $c'_i = 0$ beyond, in each of
  the three cases, and $F_{c'}(x, z) = F_c(z, x)$ for all integers $x$ and $z$, as for any two lists
  whose degrees round up to the same even number $D$ and whose coefficients are reflected at $D$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \uses{lem:squarefree_binaryFormPoly}
  \leanok
  Without a reversal the check returns $c$ and $d$. In each case of a reversal the list that the C
  code builds by its swaps is the reflection (with $c_{d+1} = 0$ in case~3); the new leading
  coefficient is $c_1$, $c_0$ or $c_1$, which is not zero, so that $c'$ has the degree $d'$, read
  off the degree of its polynomial (Lemma~\ref{lem:squarefree_binaryFormPoly}), and $D' = D$; and
  $F_{c'}(x, z) = \sum_i c_{D-i}\, x^i z^{D-i} = F_c(z, x)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[The points of a reversed search]\label{def:swapPoint}
  \lean{Ratpoints.Sieve.swapPoint}
  \leanok
  The point of the caller's curve for the point $(a, y, b)$ of the reversed curve: $(b, y, a)$ for
  $a \ge 0$ and $(-b, y, -a)$ for $a < 0$, the map that the exact check of a survivor applies after
  a reversal, as does its reference, the check of a numerator, and whose images of the caller's
  points at infinity the points at infinity deliver (Definitions~\ref{def:accepted},
  \ref{def:checkNum} and~\ref{def:pointsAtInfinity}, when their flag of the reversal is set). It is
  also the map that the mapped call of the callback applies, branch by branch
  (Definition~\ref{def:delivery}). Its negations stay in the range of a \verb|long|, since
  $|a| \le H$, $1 \le b \le H$ and $H < 2^{63}$ (the lemma on negations of
  Definition~\ref{def:useCLong}).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The reversal is a bijection of the points]\label{lem:swapPoint_bijection}
  \uses{def:swapPoint, def:IsPoint, def:bruteForce, thm:bruteForce}
  \lean{Ratpoints.Sieve.isPoint_swapPoint, Ratpoints.Sieve.swapPoint_swapPoint, Ratpoints.Sieve.swapPoint_inj, Ratpoints.IsNormalizedPair, Ratpoints.IsNormalizedPair.swapPoint, Ratpoints.Sieve.perm_map_swapPoint_bruteForce, List.perm_map_of_nodup}
  \leanok

\end{proof}

stage~F1 adds the number of primes examined and the composite moduli (Chapter~\ref{chap:tables}).

\section{The command-line program and the differential tests}\label{sec:setupCli}

\verb|ratpoints-lean| reads \verb|-k| and \verb|-K|, the last of them winning, and \verb|-s| and
\verb|-S| with its optional argument, a C \verb|long| when the next word exists and does not start
with \verb|-|, the last of them winning, into the options, as \verb|main.c| does
(\csource{main.c}{594}{601}, \csource{main.c}{638}{649}), and it refuses a form that is not
squarefree with error~8 (\csource{main.c}{256}{261}, \csource{main.c}{1047}{1047}), which the
reading of the command line does not meet. The nodes of the failures and of the command line in
Chapter~\ref{chap:spec} (Definitions~\ref{def:cliFailure} and~\ref{def:cliInput}) hold these lines,
the node of the program (Definition~\ref{def:cliRun}) the call of \verb|find_points| and the exit
status~$0$ (\csource{main.c}{251}{251}, \csource{main.c}{284}{284}), and the order of the printed
points is that of Theorem~\ref{thm:findPoints_order}. It reads the options of the output since
stage~G (Section~\ref{sec:outputCli}) and those of the search region and the denominator range,
\verb|-l -u -dl -du|, since stage~H (Section~\ref{sec:regionCli}), so that every option of ratpoints
is read. At the end of stage~D the differential tests:
\begin{itemize}
  \item compared in the suite \verb|test4| 548 of the 582 runs of \verb|test4.sh|, 261 of them by
    their points, each in its order: the runs whose only options of the search are \verb|-k|,
    \verb|-K|, \verb|-s| and \verb|-S| by their points (nine with \verb|-S|, among them the
    regression tests of the depth of the Sturm step), the 11 refusals with error~8 (three of them
    runs without \verb|-q|, then compared by the message at the end of the report, since stage~G as
    whole reports, Section~\ref{sec:outputTests}), and the two runs with a malformed argument of
    \verb|-S|, which fail with error~6 as the C program does; 21 runs stayed skipped for their
    filtered output and 13 for their height, which the suite \verb|test4high| compares;
  \item compared 16 of the 21 runs of \verb|test3.sh|, the refusal among them, the run with
    \verb|-k| by its points, and the run at the height $300000$, above the largest height of the
    tests, on which both programs print nothing;
  \item compared in the random suite the inputs that the C program refuses; searched in the suite
    of parameters \verb|0 1 1| in place of \verb|0 0 1|, which both programs refuse; compared in
    the errors suite (then 253 cases) \verb|-k|, \verb|-K|, \verb|-s|, \verb|-S| and \verb|-S 5|,
    error~8, \verb|-s| on a form that is not squarefree and the order of \verb|-s| and \verb|-S|;
    and gave in the fuzz \verb|-S| random arguments, on forms that are squarefree and on forms that
    are not;
  \item compared every point list in its order, also where ratpoints visits the denominators
    divisor by divisor (Section~\ref{sec:denCli}).
\end{itemize}
Sections~\ref{sec:outputTests} and~\ref{sec:regionTests} give the counts after stages~G and~H.

\section{Deviations}\label{sec:setupDeviations}

Each component of the set-up differs from the C code in a stated way, recorded in its
\verb|Deviation:| paragraph:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the three functions of the library, \verb|find_points|, \verb|find_points_work| and
    \verb|find_points_work_1|, as one;
  \item the refusals of the program, in its order and with its errors, for the library's codes and
    order: the library tests the leading zeros before the height, and refuses as bad arguments a
    degree $0$ and inputs that the program never passes, of which the model refuses only search
    intervals that are not well formed, with error~9 (Definition~\ref{def:searchIntervals}); a
    natural number for the height, a height of $2^{63}$ or more, which is not a \verb|long|, refused
    with error~4, as the program refuses it when it reads it;
  \item the options as a structure: Booleans for the bits \verb|RATPOINTS_NO_REVERSE|,
    \verb|RATPOINTS_NO_CHECK| and \verb|RATPOINTS_NO_Y| of the flags that \verb|main.c| clears,
    whose Jacobi switch is that of the sieving parameters, an integer for \verb|args->sturm|, and
    the search region of Chapter~\ref{chap:region}; the bit \verb|RATPOINTS_NO_REVERSE_AUTO| as the
    switch of the reversal of the normalized options cleared (Definition~\ref{def:normalize});
  \item a list of integers for the array of GMP integers of the coefficients, a new list for the
    reversal in place, and nothing written back to the caller but the three bits of the flags that
    the program reads and the search intervals (Definition~\ref{def:Outcome}): the reversed
    coefficients and the degree stay unwritten, so that there is no state between calls for the
    reset of the flags to clear; the square test of $c_d$ made where it is used
    (\verb|Setup.reversal|, \verb|Sieve.pointsAtInfinity|, \verb|searchPoints|), for the flag
    \verb|lcfsq|, which the C code computes before the reversal check, and the test on
    \verb|point_at_infty| of the 2-adic exit computed again;
  \item the rows of the chain as arrays of their coefficients up to their degree, for the table of
    $(d + 1)^2$ GMP integers and the array of degrees, with no \verb|mpz_init| and \verb|mpz_clear|;
    the rows handed back, and $\mathrm{none}$ for the return of $-1$, where
    \verb|_ratpoints_compute_sturm| keeps its table for the rest of the Sturm step; local values for
    \verb|work[0]|, \verb|work[1]| and \verb|work[2]|; Lean's division for \verb|mpz_fdiv_q|, the
    divisions being exact;
  \item no array \verb|c_long|, the flag of the \verb|long|s returned, and the loop that stops at
    the first coefficient that is not a \verb|long| as a test of all of them
    (\verb|Setup.useCLong|); Lean's truncating division for C's, and products for the shifts in the
    helper \verb|mod| (\verb|Sieve.modLong|);
  \item of \verb|examine_prime| what the table, its last entry, the coefficients modulo $p$ that it
    hands back, the exit, its codes and the density need: its reciprocal \verb|magic|, its bias and
    its inverse \verb|rbainv| are set, with its formulas, in the sieve entry of the prime that the
    model makes (\verb|makeModulus?|); one loop over the residues, where the C code has one for each
    branch of the schedule of Horner's rule; the table and the coefficients fresh arrays and the
    entry a fresh value, returned, for the buffers that the C code takes them from and the entry and
    the array of its caller where it stores them;
  \item the arguments of \verb|sieving_info| that serve the choice of the moduli (the mean number of
    bits per word, whether the number of primes was left to the library, the 2-adic classes) are not
    modelled; the exits return the delivery as it came, where \verb|sieving_info| returns the prime
    and \verb|find_points_work_1| the count $0$;
  \item integers for the \verb|long|s, with the range obligations of the helper \verb|mod| and of
    the negations of the map stated as lemmas; no failure for lack of memory; no output under
    \verb|RATPOINTS_VERBOSE| or \verb|DEBUG|.
\end{itemize}
The lines of the set-up that are neither modelled nor left to a later stage are excluded in
\verb|correspondence/ratpoints.toml|: the output under \verb|RATPOINTS_VERBOSE| and \verb|DEBUG| and
the instrumentation of the development, the management of memory and its failures, the input fields
that \verb|find_points_work| saves and puts back, the checks of arguments that only a caller of the
library can fail, and the threads; so the record of \verb|find_points_work_1| up to the run lacks
only what belongs to stage~I, now that stages~E, F1, F2, F3, F4 and~H have recorded their lines
(Chapters~\ref{chap:denominators}, \ref{chap:tables}, \ref{chap:stages}, \ref{chap:packing},
\ref{chap:rows} and~\ref{chap:region}, the fifth with the exclusions of the buffers of the tables
and of the functions that set them up and free them, Section~\ref{sec:rowsDeviations}). Stage~G
records the macros of the flags and of the return codes in \verb|ratpoints.h|
(Chapter~\ref{chap:output}), and stage~H \verb|RATPOINTS_NO_REVERSE_AUTO|, with the normalized
options (Definition~\ref{def:normalize}).

\section{The evidence}\label{sec:setup-evidence}

Besides the proofs:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a reading of the C code of the set-up at the pin, with a build that traces every return of
    \verb|find_points_work_1|: \verb|test4.sh| and 3012 random inputs under four sets of options
    reach only the refusals and exits of Section~\ref{sec:setupC}, the stop that the callback
    requests (stage~G) and the return when the Sturm step leaves nothing to search (stage~H); the
    refusal predicates of the program, with the Sturm step on and off and with and without the
    reversal, agree with Lemma~\ref{lem:squarefree_binaryFormPoly} and Theorem~\ref{thm:refusals} on
    9900 coefficient lists under each set and on 24360 further runs; the three cases of the reversal
    on 31164 lists, each with $F'(x, z) = F(z, x)$ and $D' = D$; the order of the output, with and
    without a reversal, on 2962 runs; the exit on curves without points modulo $3$, $5$, $7$ and
    $131$, and its soundness on 33761 pairs of a curve with a point and a prime; a transcription of
    the remainder chain against sympy and PARI on 24000 polynomials and against an instrumented
    build of the C code, row by row, on 3000; and the helper \verb|mod| under the

  \item before the stage, a sketch of the model in Lean, checked against the C program on 2600 runs,
