% that is not squarefree with the remainder chain of sturm.c, the reversal with its three cases, the
% coefficients as longs, the exit when the curve has no points modulo a prime, and the points at
% infinity in all cases. Proposed names are set with \lname and become \lean once the declarations

\chapter{The set-up}\label{chap:setup}

Stage~D models what ratpoints does with its input before the sieve: it drops the leading zero
coefficients, refuses a binary form that is not squarefree, reverses the polynomial in three cases,
reads the coefficients as \verb|long|s where they fit, ends the search when the curve has no points
modulo one of the primes it examines, and delivers the points at infinity, after a reversal as
affine points of the caller's curve. With it the refusal theorem of Section~\ref{sec:refusals} gets
its proof: with the Sturm step on, as in a run of the program without options,
$\mathrm{findPoints}$ refuses $(c, H)$ exactly when $c$ has fewer than two or more than
$\mathrm{maxDegree} + 1$ entries, $H = 0$, or $\Phi_c$ is not squarefree; with the Sturm step off,
it refuses exactly what the program refuses under \verb|-s|.

The reversal is the transformation of Chapter~\ref{chap:sieve}: it exchanges $x$ and $z$ in the
binary form, and the map that the C code applies to every point it finds of the reversed polynomial
is a bijection onto the points of the caller's curve that preserves the box. So after a reversal the
output is the list of the brute-force reference for the reversed polynomial with each point mapped
back, a permutation of the reference; the equation of Theorem~\ref{thm:findPoints_eq_bruteForce}
becomes the equation of the points at infinity and the run, for any polynomial and its degree, and
membership and no duplicates hold for every option. The set-up has been the most error-prone part
of ratpoints: the fixes of 2.2.2 include a reversal that marked a cubic reversed and left it as it
was, and those of 2.2.4 a search that returned before the points at infinity when the Sturm step
found no real point in the search region.

$\mathrm{findPoints}$ gains an argument for the options of the library that change the refusals or
the order of the output, and the program gains the options that set them: \verb|-k| and \verb|-K|,
which forbid and allow the reversal, and \verb|-s| and \verb|-S|, which switch the Sturm step off and
on. Of the Sturm step, stage~D models the test for a repeated factor, its first half; the rest,
which restricts the numerators to the intervals where $f \ge 0$, is stage~H.

\section{What the C code does before the sieve}\label{sec:setupC}

For the coefficient list $c_0, \dots, c_n$ and the height $H$, the program checks the number of
coefficients and the height before it calls the library (\csource{main.c}{362}{380}), and
\verb|find_points_work| refuses a degree outside $0, \dots, \mathrm{maxDegree}$
(\csource{find_points.c}{4557}{4558}). Then \verb|find_points_work_1|:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item drops the leading zero coefficients and refuses the form as not squarefree
    (\verb|RATPOINTS_NON_SQUAREFREE|) when that lowers $\lceil n/2 \rceil$
    (\csource{find_points.c}{4624}{4633}): for the degree $d$ that is left and $N = 2 \lceil n/2
    \rceil$ this is $d + 2 \le N$, that is $Z^2 \mid \Phi_c$; it covers the zero polynomial and every
    list whose only nonzero entry is $c_0$, so that the refusal of degree $0$ which follows is never
    met from the program; it records whether $c_d$ is a square (\csource{find_points.c}{4634}{4634}),
    caps the parameters and refuses $H < 1$, which the program never passes;
  \item unless the caller forbids it (\verb|RATPOINTS_NO_REVERSE|, the program's \verb|-k|) or gives
    search intervals or bounds for the denominators (\csource{find_points.c}{4686}{4687}, stage~H),
    checks whether to reverse the polynomial (\csource{find_points.c}{4732}{4803}): in case~1, $d$
    even and $c_0 = 0$, it refuses the form as not squarefree if also $c_1 = 0$, since then
    $X^2 \mid \Phi_c$, and otherwise reverses $c_0, \dots, c_d$ and lowers the degree to $d - 1$; in
    case~2, $d$ even, $c_d$ a square and $c_0$ not, it reverses $c_0, \dots, c_d$; in case~3, $d$
    odd, $|c_d| \ne 1$, $c_0 = 0$ and $|c_1| = 1$, it reverses $c_1, \dots, c_d$; in each case the
    binary form $F'$ of the new list has the degree $D$ of $F$ and $F'(x, z) = F(z, x)$, and the new
    leading coefficient is not zero;
  \item records whether the coefficients fit into \verb|long|s (\csource{find_points.c}{4814}{4823}),
    so that \verb|examine_prime| reduces them modulo $p$ with the helper \verb|mod|
    (\csource{find_points.c}{477}{492}) rather than with GMP;
  \item with the Sturm step on (\verb|args->sturm| $\ge 0$, by default $10$; the program's \verb|-s|
    sets $-1$, and \verb|-S| the default or a depth), computes the remainder chain of $f$ and $f'$
    (\csource{sturm.c}{99}{163}) and refuses the form as not squarefree when a remainder is zero
    (\csource{find_points.c}{4857}{4863}, \csource{find_points.c}{4879}{4879}); the rest of the step
    is stage~H, and so is its exit when $f$ is negative on the whole search region, which changes no
    output, since the box has no affine point then, and the return after the points at infinity
    when the step leaves nothing to search (\csource{find_points.c}{5106}{5106});
  \item decides whether there are points at infinity (\csource{find_points.c}{4894}{4899}) and sets up
    the tests on the denominators and the 2-adic information, with an exit of its own that changes
    no output either (stage~E);
  \item examines the primes, and the first prime modulo which the curve has no points, neither a
    residue $x$ with $f(x)$ a square nor a point at infinity, ends the search with no points
    (\csource{find_points.c}{1761}{1762}, \csource{find_points.c}{2608}{2614},
    \csource{find_points.c}{2994}{2998}, \csource{find_points.c}{4992}{5012}); the primes it
    examines are the first \verb|num_primes| of the table, and more when the number was left to the
    library;
  \item delivers the points at infinity, as $(0, y, 1)$ after a reversal
    (\csource{find_points.c}{5068}{5105}), and runs the sieve, whose check of a survivor $(a, b)$ of
    the reversed polynomial delivers $(b, y, a)$ for $a \ge 0$ and $(-b, y, -a)$ for $a < 0$
    (\csource{find_points.c}{5250}{5338}).
\end{enumerate}
The program turns \verb|RATPOINTS_NON_SQUAREFREE| into its error~8, the message
\verb|Polynomial is not square-free.| and the exit status~8 (\csource{main.c}{256}{261},
\csource{main.c}{1047}{1047}). No point is printed before it, since all three refusals come before
the first point: with \verb|-q| nothing at all, and without \verb|-q| the banner and the curve
(\csource{main.c}{215}{220}), then the height bound, or under \verb|-v|, for the refusals of steps~2
and~4, the report of the library in its place (for that of step~1 nothing, since it comes before the
report). With the Sturm step on, the C code refuses the form exactly when $\Phi_c$ is not
squarefree: $Z^2 \mid \Phi_c$ in step~1, $X^2 \mid \Phi_c$ for even $d$ in step~2, and a repeated
factor of the polynomial searched in step~4; with the Sturm step off only steps~1 and~2 remain, and
step~2 only when the reversal check runs. After a reversal the program prints the points of the
reversed polynomial in the order of its search, each mapped back; where the denominators ascend,
this is the list of the brute-force reference for the reversed polynomial, mapped. The exit of
step~6 never fires after a reversal (every case leaves the caller's curve a point of height~$1$),
and when it fires the curve has no points at all, so the primes it examines cannot change the
output.

\section{The options}\label{sec:options}

\begin{definition}[The options of a search]\label{def:Options}
  Proposed names: \lname{Ratpoints.Options}, \lname{Ratpoints.defaultSturm}.
  The options of the library that change the refusals or the order of the output, an argument of
  $\mathrm{findPoints}$ whose defaults are those of a run of the program without options: whether
  the set-up may reverse the polynomial (the bit \verb|RATPOINTS_NO_REVERSE| of the flags clear),
  and \verb|args->sturm|, the depth of the Sturm step, negative for none, with the default
  $\mathrm{defaultSturm} = 10$ (\verb|RATPOINTS_DEFAULT_STURM|, \csource{ratpoints.h}{341}{343};
  the program's defaults \csource{main.c}{348}{348}, \csource{main.c}{352}{352}). Stage~D reads only
  whether the depth is negative, as the switch of the test for a repeated factor; stage~H reads the
  depth itself, and stages~G and~H add the options of the output and the search region.
\end{definition}

The options are not parameters: the parameters are the free choices that change neither the points
nor their order (Definition~\ref{def:Params}), while the reversal changes the order and, without the
Sturm step, the refusals. $\mathrm{defaultSturm}$ joins the constants of
\verb|Ratpoints/Model/Constants.lean|, and no proof depends on its value. In ratpoints~3.1.1 the
depth of the Sturm step changes only the speed: the intervals that it keeps hold every point of
the box, which stage~H proves; so the model of stage~D gives the points of the program for every
depth.

\section{The squarefree binary form}\label{sec:squarefree}

The refusals speak about $\Phi_c$, the C code about $f = \sum_i c_i X^i$, its degree $d$ and its
reversal. Chapter~\ref{chap:spec} names the facts that connect them; Mathlib has none of them, and
they are proved here at Mathlib's generality, as candidates for \verb|Ratpoints/Mathlib|.

\begin{lemma}[Homogenizations and squarefreeness]\label{lem:homogenize}
  \lean{MvPolynomial.IsHomogeneous.of_mul_left, Polynomial.squarefree_homogenize_iff, Polynomial.homogenize_reflect, MulEquiv.squarefree_iff, Polynomial.squarefree_homogenize_reflect_iff}
  \leanok
  Over a commutative semiring without zero divisors, a factor of a nonzero homogeneous polynomial
  is homogeneous. For a polynomial $p$ over a domain and $m \ge \deg p$, the homogenization of $p$
  of degree $m$ is squarefree if and only if $p$ is squarefree and $m \le \deg p + 1$. Over a
  commutative semiring, the homogenization of degree $m$ of the reflection of $p$ in degree $m$ is
  that of $p$ with the two variables exchanged, for every $p$ and $m$; squarefreeness is preserved
  by multiplicative equivalences; so the reflection does not change whether the homogenization is
  squarefree.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  In a product over a semiring without zero divisors the lowest degrees add up (the order of the
  power series) and so do the highest (the total degree), so a factor of a homogeneous polynomial
  has the two equal. If $m \ge \deg p + 2$, the square of $Z$ divides the homogenization, and if
  $p = a^2 q$, the homogenizations of $a$ and $q$ give a square factor. Conversely, a factor $A$ of
  the homogenization with $A^2$ dividing it is homogeneous; $Z$ does not divide it, since
  $m \le \deg p + 1$ and the homogenization of degree $\deg p$ takes the leading coefficient of $p$
  at $(1, 0)$; and $A$ with $Z = 1$, squared, divides $p$, so it is a unit, a constant, and so is
  $A$. The reflection compares the coefficients of the two homogenizations, which agree for every
  $p$ and $m$, and exchanging the variables is a ring automorphism.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The refusal predicate in the terms of the C code]\label{lem:squarefree_binaryFormPoly}
  \uses{def:binaryFormPoly, lem:homogenize}
  \lean{Ratpoints.poly, Ratpoints.getD_degree_ne_zero_of_pos, Ratpoints.natDegree_poly, Ratpoints.binaryFormPoly_eq_homogenize, Ratpoints.squarefree_binaryFormPoly_iff, Ratpoints.not_squarefree_binaryFormPoly_of_lt, Ratpoints.not_squarefree_binaryFormPoly_of_getD_zero}
  \leanok
  For $d \ge 1$ the entry $c_d$ is not zero; the polynomial $f = \sum_i c_i X^i \in \QQ[X]$ has the
  degree $d$ of $c$, and $\Phi_c$ is its homogenization of degree $N$; so $\Phi_c$ is squarefree if
  and only if $N \le d + 1$ and $f$ is squarefree. In particular $d + 2 \le N$ gives a form that is
  not squarefree, and so does $c_0 = c_1 = 0$, for every degree.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The entries above $d$ are $0$, and $c_d \ne 0$ for $d \ge 1$, so $f$ has degree $d$, and $\Phi_c$
  is the homogenization of $f$ of degree $N \ge n \ge d$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:homogenize} gives the
  equivalence; $d + 2 \le N$ violates $N \le d + 1$; and $c_0 = c_1 = 0$ gives $X^2 \mid f$, where
  $X$ is not a unit, so that $f$ is not squarefree, $f = 0$ included.
\end{proof}

On every input with $N \le d + 1$, $D = N$, and $\Phi_c$ evaluates to $F_c$
(Definition~\ref{def:binaryFormPoly}); so the predicate of the refusals and the form of the points
fit together on every input that passes the test of the leading zeros, in particular on every input
that is not refused.

\section{The remainder chain}\label{sec:chain}

\begin{definition}[The remainder chain]\label{def:sturmChain}
  \lean{Ratpoints.Sturm.reduce, Ratpoints.Sturm.chain, Ratpoints.Sturm.fullReduce, Ratpoints.Sturm.normalize, Ratpoints.Sturm.IsChain, Ratpoints.Sturm.IsChainResult, Ratpoints.Sturm.chain_spec}
  \leanok
  For the coefficients $c_0, \dots, c_d$ of $f$, $d \ge 1$ and $c_d \ne 0$: $s_0 = f$, $s_1 = f'$ (the
  coefficients $(i + 1) c_{i+1}$), and for $k = 2, \dots, d$ the remainder $t$ of $s_{k-2}$ by
  $s_{k-1}$ as \verb|sturm.c| computes it in integers: starting from $t = s_{k-2}$, while
  $\deg t \ge \deg s_{k-1}$, $t$ becomes $u t - v X^{\deg t - \deg s_{k-1}} s_{k-1}$, where $u$ and
  $v$ are the leading coefficients of $s_{k-1}$ and $t$ divided by their positive $\gcd$ and signed
  so that $u > 0$, and its zero leading coefficients are dropped; the chain ends with
  $\mathrm{none}$ if $t$ becomes zero, and otherwise $s_k$ is $-t$ divided by the $\gcd$ of its
  coefficients, the chain ending when $s_k$ is constant (\csource{sturm.c}{99}{163}). For $d = 1$
  the loop over $k$ does not run, and the chain is $f, f'$, with $f' = c_1$ constant; for $d \ge 2$ it
  ends at its \verb|break| at a constant $s_k$ or at the return of $\mathrm{none}$, never at its
  bound, since $\deg s_k \le d - k$. The loops are those of the C code: the loop over $k$, the
  reduction while the degree of $t$ is at least that of $s_{k-1}$, the drop of the zero
  coefficients, and the loop for the $\gcd$ of the coefficients, which stops when the $\gcd$ is $1$.
  Each $s_k$ is the array of its coefficients up to its degree, which stands for a row of the C
  code's table of $(d + 1)^2$ GMP integers together with its entry in the array of degrees; one
  reduction step, $\mathrm{reduce}$, a helper inside the lines of the chain, has no C range of its
  own, as \verb|Sieve.evalMod| has none inside those of the table of a prime
(Definition~\ref{def:examinePrimeExit}). The
  contract, with Lean core's program logic: the loops end by their measures, the sizes of the
  remainders and of the arrays whose zeros are dropped; and the result is a chain in the sense of
  the named predicate $\mathrm{IsChain}$ (its first two elements are $f$ and $f'$, each further one is
  the full reduction ($\mathrm{fullReduce}$) of the element two before by the element before,
  negated and divided by the $\gcd$ of its coefficients ($\mathrm{normalize}$), every element has a
  nonzero leading coefficient, and the sizes decrease from $s_1$ on) whose last element is
  constant, or $\mathrm{none}$, and then a chain in this sense exists whose last element has
  positive degree and reduces the element before it to zero.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The chain detects a repeated factor]\label{lem:sturmChain_squarefree}
  \uses{def:sturmChain, lem:squarefree_binaryFormPoly}
  \lean{Ratpoints.Sturm.IsChain.exists_mul_eq, Ratpoints.Sturm.IsChain.natDegree_lt, Ratpoints.Sturm.run_chain_eq_none_iff, isCoprime_iff_of_mul_eq_mul_sub_mul}
  \leanok
  For $d \ge 1$ and $c_d \ne 0$, the chain is $\mathrm{none}$ if and only if $f$ is not squarefree
  over $\QQ$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The polynomial of an array of coefficients is $\mathrm{poly}$ of its list
  (Lemma~\ref{lem:squarefree_binaryFormPoly}). Each $s_k$, $k \ge 2$, satisfies
  $A s_{k-2} = Q s_{k-1} - g s_k$ with integers $A \ge 1$ and $g \ge 1$ and some $Q \in \QQ[X]$, read
  off the arrays coefficient by coefficient; for units $a$ and $b$ of a commutative ring,
  $a x = q y - b z$ makes $y$ and $z$ coprime exactly when $x$ and $y$ are, so coprimality passes
  along the chain. A zero remainder after $s_{k-1}$ of degree at least $1$ makes $s_{k-1}$ a common
  factor of positive degree, and a nonzero constant makes the pair coprime. Over $\QQ$, $f$ and $f'$
  are coprime exactly when $f$ is separable, which in characteristic $0$ is squarefreeness. The
  converse of the contract's clause for $\mathrm{none}$ follows from this lemma: such a chain makes
  its element of positive degree a common factor of $f$ and $f'$, so that $f$ is not squarefree, and
  the chain is $\mathrm{none}$.
\end{proof}

The relation with positive $A$ and $g$ and the decreasing degrees is also what stage~H needs of the
chain, whose signs Sturm's theorem reads; so the chain is computed as the C code computes it, row
by row, and not only its verdict.

\section{The reversal}\label{sec:reversal}

\begin{definition}[The reversal check]\label{def:reversal}
  \lean{Ratpoints.Setup.isSquare, Ratpoints.Setup.reversal, Ratpoints.Refusal, Ratpoints.Refusal.notSquarefree}
  \leanok
  For the switch of the reversal and the coefficients $c$ of degree $d$, the leading zeros dropped:
  if the reversal is forbidden, $c$ itself; otherwise, for even $d$ with $c_0 = 0$, the refusal if
  $c_1 = 0$, and otherwise the list $c_d, \dots, c_1, c_0$ with the degree $d - 1$ (case~1); for
  even $d$ with $c_d$ a square and $c_0$ not, the list $c_d, \dots, c_0$ (case~2); for odd $d$ with
  $|c_d| \ne 1$, $c_0 = 0$ and $|c_1| = 1$, the list $c_0, c_d, \dots, c_1$ (case~3); and otherwise
  $c$ itself; with a flag that says whether the list is reversed
  (\csource{find_points.c}{4732}{4803}). The squares are those of \verb|mpz_perfect_square_p|: $0$
  and the squares of the positive integers. The switch is the test of the flags that guards the C
  block; of its two bits the model has the caller's, \verb|RATPOINTS_NO_REVERSE|, and not
  \verb|RATPOINTS_NO_REVERSE_AUTO|, which a search region or a denominator range sets (stage~H).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The reversal exchanges $x$ and $z$]\label{lem:reversal_swap}
  \uses{def:reversal, def:binaryForm}
  \lean{Ratpoints.Setup.getD_eq_of_reversal_eq_ok, Ratpoints.Setup.degree_eq_of_reversal_eq_ok, Ratpoints.Setup.eq_of_reversal_eq_ok_false, Ratpoints.Setup.binaryForm_eq_of_reversal_eq_ok, Ratpoints.binaryForm_eq_of_getD_eq_getD_sub}
  \leanok
  For $d$ the degree of $c$: if the check returns the list $c'$ with the degree $d'$, then $d'$ is
  the degree of $c'$, and without a reversal $c' = c$ and $d' = d$; if it returns them as reversed,
  then $c'$ is the reflection of $c$ in degree $D$, $c'_i = c_{D-i}$ for $i \le D$ and $c'_i = 0$
  beyond, in each of the three cases, and $F_{c'}(x, z) = F_c(z, x)$ for all integers $x$ and $z$,
  as for any two lists whose degrees round up to the same even number $D$ and whose coefficients
  are reflected at $D$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \uses{lem:squarefree_binaryFormPoly}
  \leanok
  Without a reversal the check returns $c$ and $d$. In each case of a reversal the list that the C
  code builds by its swaps is the reflection (with $c_{d+1} = 0$ in case~3); the new leading
  coefficient is $c_1$, $c_0$ or $c_1$, which is not zero, so that $c'$ has the degree $d'$, read off
  the degree of its polynomial (Lemma~\ref{lem:squarefree_binaryFormPoly}), and $D' = D$; and
  $F_{c'}(x, z) = \sum_i c_{D-i}\, x^i z^{D-i} = F_c(z, x)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[The points of a reversed search]\label{def:swapPoint}
  \lean{Ratpoints.Sieve.swapPoint}
  \leanok
  The point of the caller's curve for the point $(a, y, b)$ of the reversed curve: $(b, y, a)$ for
  $a \ge 0$ and $(-b, y, -a)$ for $a < 0$, the map that the check of a survivor and the points at
  infinity apply after a reversal (Definitions~\ref{def:checkNum} and~\ref{def:pointsAtInfinity},
  when their flag of the reversal is set). Its negations stay in the range of a \verb|long|, since
  $|a| \le H$, $1 \le b \le H$ and $H < 2^{63}$ (the lemma on negations of
  Definition~\ref{def:useCLong}).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The reversal is a bijection of the points]\label{lem:swapPoint_bijection}
  \uses{def:swapPoint, def:IsPoint, def:bruteForce, thm:bruteForce}
  \lean{Ratpoints.Sieve.isPoint_swapPoint, Ratpoints.Sieve.swapPoint_swapPoint_of_isPoint, Ratpoints.Sieve.perm_map_swapPoint_bruteForce, List.perm_map_of_nodup}
  \leanok
  For lists $c$ and $c'$ with $F_{c'}(x, z) = F_c(z, x)$ for all $x$ and $z$, the map sends a
  normalized point of height at most $H$ of $c'$ to one of $c$, and back, the two maps being inverse
  on these points; so $\mathrm{bruteForce}(c', H)$, mapped, is a permutation of
  $\mathrm{bruteForce}(c, H)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  $F_c(-b, -a) = F_c(b, a)$, since $D$ is even, so the equation holds. For $a > 0$ the image has
  $z = a > 0$; for $a < 0$, $z = -a > 0$; for $a = 0$ the normalization of $(0, y, b)$ forces
  $b = 1$, whose image $(1, y, 0)$ is the caller's point at infinity; $|x| = b$ and $z = |a|$, so
  the box goes onto the box. On points the map is its own inverse, so it is injective there, and
  both lists have no duplicates (Theorem~\ref{thm:bruteForce}) and the same members.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The reversal and squarefreeness]\label{lem:reversal_squarefree}
  \uses{lem:reversal_swap, lem:squarefree_binaryFormPoly}
  \lean{Ratpoints.Setup.squarefree_binaryFormPoly_iff_of_reversal_eq_ok, Polynomial.squarefree_homogenize_iff_squarefree_reflect}
  \leanok
  For $d$ the degree of $c$: if $N \le d + 1$ and the check returns the list $c'$, reversed or not,
  then $\Phi_c$ is squarefree if and only if the polynomial of $c'$ is.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Without a reversal $c' = c$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:squarefree_binaryFormPoly} gives the claim. After a
  reversal the polynomial of $c'$ is the reflection of $f$ in degree $D = N$ and has the degree
  $d' \in \{D - 1, D\}$; by Lemma~\ref{lem:homogenize} the reflection does not change whether the
  homogenization of degree $D$ is squarefree, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:squarefree_binaryFormPoly} that
  homogenization is squarefree exactly when the polynomial of $c'$ is.
\end{proof}

The check of a numerator (Definition~\ref{def:checkNum}) and the points at infinity
(Definition~\ref{def:pointsAtInfinity}) take the flag \verb|RATPOINTS_REVERSED| as an argument, before
the numerator, and map each point they deliver by $\mathrm{swapPoint}$ when it is set, where the C
code does; the lines they cite already hold the reversal, so their ranges stay, and their
deviations lose the reversal. The components between the check and the run, \verb|walkWord|,
\verb|scanPiece|, \verb|siftPiece|, \verb|sift| and \verb|sieveRun| (Definitions~\ref{def:walkWord},
\ref{def:scanPiece}, \ref{def:siftPiece}, \ref{def:sift} and~\ref{def:sieveRun}), pass the flag on:
the contracts of the walk, the scan and the piece keep their proofs with the check as it is now; the
postconditions of the contracts of \verb|sift| and \verb|sieveRun| and of their equations gain the
map, through the equation of the flagged check with the old one; and the sieve and the check of
stage~C1, their reference, stay without the flag, so that the equation of
Lemma~\ref{lem:sift_eq_checkDen} maps their side.

\section{The coefficients as longs and the exit}\label{sec:longsExit}

\begin{definition}[The coefficients as longs]\label{def:useCLong}
  \lean{Ratpoints.fitsLong, Ratpoints.Setup.useCLong, Ratpoints.Sieve.modLong, Ratpoints.Sieve.modLong_eq_emod, Ratpoints.fitsLong_sixteen_mul, Ratpoints.fitsLong_add_sixteen_mul, Ratpoints.fitsLong_neg, Ratpoints.Setup.fitsLong_getD_of_useCLong}
  \leanok
  Whether every coefficient $c_0, \dots, c_d$ is a C \verb|long|, $-2^{63} \le c_i < 2^{63}$
  (\csource{find_points.c}{4599}{4600}, \csource{find_points.c}{4814}{4823}); and the helper
  \verb|mod| of the C code, which reduces a \verb|long| $a$ modulo $b > 0$ by subtracting $8b$, $4b$,
  $2b$ and $b$ where it can, after adding $16b$ to a negative $a \ge -16b$, and by C's division, which
  truncates, for $a$ outside $[-16b, 16b)$ (\csource{find_points.c}{477}{492}). For every integer
  $a$ and $b \ge 0$ its result is the Euclidean remainder of $a$ by $b$, so the two paths of the table
  give the same result; and for $b < 2^{59}$ the values that it computes and that could leave the
  range of a \verb|long|, $16b$, its negation, and $a + 16b$ for $-16b \le a < 0$, stay in it, its
  other values lying in $(-b, 16b)$, while its inputs are \verb|long|s by the flag; the negation of an
  integer of absolute value below $2^{63}$ is a \verb|long|, which also covers the negations of the
  map of a reversed search (Definition~\ref{def:swapPoint}).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The table of a prime, or the exit]\label{def:examinePrimeExit}
  \uses{def:examinePrime, def:useCLong}
  \lean{Ratpoints.Sieve.examinePrime?, Ratpoints.Sieve.eq_examinePrime_of_examinePrime?_eq_some, Ratpoints.Sieve.examinePrime?_eq_none_iff, Ratpoints.Sieve.examinePrime?_eq_examinePrime?_false}
  \leanok
  The function \verb|examine_prime| as the C code has it, for the flag of the \verb|long|s: the
  coefficients reduced modulo $p$ on the path that the flag chooses, with \verb|mod| for
  \verb|long|s and with the Euclidean remainder for GMP integers, two paths with the same result; the
  table of the residues $x$ with $f(x)$ a square modulo $p$; its last entry, whether there is a point
  at infinity modulo $p$; and no table when the curve has no points modulo $p$, the exit
  (\csource{find_points.c}{1703}{1749}, \csource{find_points.c}{1761}{1765},
  \csource{find_points.c}{1795}{1824}). Otherwise its table is that of
  Definition~\ref{def:examinePrime}, which is the reference of this one and has given it its C
  ranges, as the sieve and the check of stage~C1 became the reference of the words; so the lemmas on
  the table and the rows hold as they are.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[A curve without points modulo $p$ has none]\label{lem:examinePrime_none}
  \uses{def:examinePrimeExit, def:IsPoint, lem:admits_examinePrime}
  \lean{Ratpoints.Sieve.not_isPoint_of_examinePrime?_eq_none, Ratpoints.Sieve.isSquare_getD_degree_of_sq_eq_binaryForm}
  \leanok
  For a prime $p$ and the degree $d$ of $c$: if the table of $p$ is none, then $c$ has no normalized
  point of any height.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  For a point $(x, y, z)$ with $p \nmid z$, the row of $z$ admits $x$
  (Lemma~\ref{lem:admits_examinePrime}), so that the table marks the residue $x z^{-1}$, at which
  $f$ takes a square value modulo $p$. For $p \mid z$, $z = 0$ included, $p \nmid x$, and
  $y^2 \equiv c_D x^D$ modulo $p$, so that $c_D$ is a square modulo $p$; for odd $d$, $c_D = 0$, and
  the entry at infinity is always set.
\end{proof}

\section{The search and the theorems}\label{sec:setupSearch}

\begin{definition}[The search of the polynomial handed on]\label{def:searchPoints}
  \uses{def:Params, def:arraySize, def:examinePrimeExit, def:useCLong, def:pointsAtInfinity,
    def:sieveRun, def:swapPoint, lem:examinePrime_none, thm:bruteForce, thm:findPoints_eq_bruteForce}
  \lean{Ratpoints.searchPoints, Ratpoints.run_searchPoints, List.eq_map_of_mapM_eq_some, List.exists_mem_of_mapM_eq_none}
  \leanok
  For the coefficients $c$ of degree $d$ that the set-up hands on, the flag of the reversal, the
  height $H$ and the parameters $P$: the table of each modulus, with the flag of the \verb|long|s,
  and no points when one of them is none (\csource{find_points.c}{2608}{2614},
  \csource{find_points.c}{2994}{2998}, \csource{find_points.c}{4992}{5012}); otherwise the points at
  infinity and the points of the denominators, with pieces of at most $\mathrm{arraySizeAt}(H)$ bit
  arrays, each mapped by $\mathrm{swapPoint}$ when the flag is set (\csource{find_points.c}{5106}{5106},
  the return when the Sturm step leaves nothing to search, is not met before stage~H). A definition
  of type \verb|Id|, which $\mathrm{findPoints}$ runs. For well-formed parameters, $H \ge 1$ and $d$
  the degree of $c$, its result is $\mathrm{bruteForce}(c, H)$, mapped by $\mathrm{swapPoint}$ when
  the flag is set, also when the exit fires, since the reference is empty then.
\end{definition}

$\mathrm{findPoints}(c, H, P, o)$ (Definition~\ref{def:findPoints}) refuses, in the order of the
program, more than $\mathrm{maxDegree} + 1$ coefficients, fewer than two and $H = 0$ (errors 3, 5
and~4), and then, with error~8, a drop of $\lceil n/2 \rceil$ by the leading zeros; it runs the
reversal check with the switch of the options, which may refuse with error~8 as well, and with the
Sturm step on refuses the form when the remainder chain of the polynomial searched is
$\mathrm{none}$; and it runs $\mathrm{searchPoints}$ on the polynomial that the check hands on. Its
node gains the uses of Definitions~\ref{def:Options}, \ref{def:reversal}, \ref{def:sturmChain}
and~\ref{def:searchPoints}, and its C ranges gain the head and the locals of
\verb|find_points_work_1| that the set-up uses and its reset of the flags
(\csource{find_points.c}{4567}{4571}, \csource{find_points.c}{4578}{4585},
\csource{find_points.c}{4606}{4606}) with its end (\csource{find_points.c}{5219}{5220}), and the Sturm
step (\csource{find_points.c}{4857}{4892}), whose
restriction of the search region stage~H takes over.

\begin{theorem}[The output of the search]\label{thm:findPoints_perm}
  \uses{def:findPoints, def:searchPoints, lem:reversal_swap, lem:swapPoint_bijection}
  Proposed names: \lname{Ratpoints.exists\_eq\_of\_findPoints\_eq\_ok},
  \lname{Ratpoints.eq\_bruteForce\_of\_findPoints\_eq\_ok\_of\_reverse\_eq\_false},
  \lname{Ratpoints.perm\_bruteForce\_of\_findPoints\_eq\_ok}.
  For well-formed parameters and any options, if $\mathrm{findPoints}(c, H, P, o)$ returns the list
  $L$, then $L$ is $\mathrm{bruteForce}(c', H)$, mapped by $\mathrm{swapPoint}$ when the reversal check
  reverses $c$ to $c'$, and $\mathrm{bruteForce}(c, H)$ when it does not, in particular when the
  reversal is forbidden; in every case $L$ is a permutation of $\mathrm{bruteForce}(c, H)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  $\mathrm{findPoints}$ returns the result of $\mathrm{searchPoints}$ on the polynomial searched
  and its degree (Lemma~\ref{lem:reversal_swap}), which is the reference for it, mapped after a
  reversal (Definition~\ref{def:searchPoints}); Lemma~\ref{lem:swapPoint_bijection} gives the
  permutation.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The refusals]\label{thm:refusals}
  \uses{def:findPoints, def:Options, def:reversal, lem:sturmChain_squarefree,
    lem:reversal_squarefree, lem:squarefree_binaryFormPoly}
  Proposed names: \lname{Ratpoints.exists\_findPoints\_eq\_error\_iff\_of\_sturm\_nonneg},
  \lname{Ratpoints.exists\_findPoints\_eq\_error\_iff\_of\_sturm\_neg},
  \lname{Ratpoints.run\_chain\_eq\_none\_iff\_of\_reversal\_eq\_ok},
  \lname{Ratpoints.Setup.one\_le\_of\_reversal\_eq\_ok}.
  For any parameters and options: with the Sturm step on ($\mathrm{sturm} \ge 0$),
  $\mathrm{findPoints}(c, H, P, o)$ refuses the input exactly when $c$ has fewer than two or more than
  $\mathrm{maxDegree} + 1$ entries, $H = 0$, or $\Phi_c$ is not squarefree. With the Sturm step off,
  it refuses exactly when $c$ has fewer than two or more than $\mathrm{maxDegree} + 1$ entries,
  $H = 0$, $d + 2 \le N$, or the reversal is allowed, $d$ is even and $c_0 = c_1 = 0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  The refusals of the leading zeros and of the reversal check stand for $Z^2 \mid \Phi_c$ and
  $X^2 \mid \Phi_c$, each of which makes $\Phi_c$ not squarefree
  (Lemma~\ref{lem:squarefree_binaryFormPoly}). When neither happens, $N \le d + 1$; since $c$ has at
  least two entries, $N \ge 2$ and so $d \ge 1$; case~1 lowers an even $d \ge 2$ to $d - 1 \ge 1$ with
  the leading coefficient $c_1 \ne 0$, and cases~2 and~3 keep $d$ with the leading coefficients
  $c_0 \ne 0$ and $c_1 = \pm 1$; so the chain of the polynomial searched is $\mathrm{none}$ exactly
  when $\Phi_c$ is not squarefree (Lemmas~\ref{lem:sturmChain_squarefree}
  and~\ref{lem:reversal_squarefree}, the latter also when the reversal is forbidden and the
  polynomial searched is $f$). Without the Sturm step only the first two remain.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:findPoints_eq_bruteForce} becomes the equation of the points at infinity and the
run of any polynomial with its degree: for moduli that are primes, an array size at least $1$ and
$H \ge 1$, they give $\mathrm{bruteForce}(c, H)$ with the flag of the reversal clear, mapped by
$\mathrm{swapPoint}$ when it is set (proposed name
\lname{Ratpoints.Sieve.pointsAtInfinity\_append\_toList\_run\_sieveRun}), on which
Definition~\ref{def:searchPoints} rests; its old statement about $\mathrm{findPoints}$ goes, with
its guard on the axioms, and its node uses Definitions~\ref{def:pointsAtInfinity},
\ref{def:sieveRun} and~\ref{def:bruteForce} in place of Definitions~\ref{def:findPoints}
and~\ref{def:Params}, since it speaks of moduli and an array size rather than of the parameters,
which come in with the search, while the node of $\mathrm{findPoints}$ uses
Definition~\ref{def:searchPoints} in place of the run, the points at infinity and the array size.
Theorem~\ref{thm:findPoints} keeps membership, no duplicates and the set of elements, for every
option, from Theorem~\ref{thm:findPoints_perm}; its equation with the reference gives way to that
theorem and its refusals to Theorem~\ref{thm:refusals}, and the guards on the axioms move to the new
theorems. Comparator certifies the two main theorems with the options argument, both refusal
theorems and \verb|wf_sieveParams|; the challenge restates $\Phi_c$ verbatim, as it restates $F_c$,
takes the options from the model, and has the refusal theorem with the Sturm step off too, so that a
$\mathrm{findPoints}$ that refused every input under \verb|-s| would not pass. Since the challenge
needs \verb|Squarefree|, which the imports of the specification do not reach, the specification and
the challenge gain the import of \verb|Mathlib.Algebra.Squarefree.Basic|, so that the challenge
still imports exactly the public imports of the specification. The structure \verb|Params| and its
well-formedness do not change.

\section{Deviations}\label{sec:setupDeviations}

Each component of the set-up differs from the C code in a stated way, recorded in its
\verb|Deviation:| paragraph:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the refusals of the program, in its order and with its errors, for the library's codes and
    order: the library tests the leading zeros before the height, and refuses as bad arguments a
    degree $0$ and inputs that the program never passes; no error~9, which the program meets only
    for such codes;
  \item the options as a structure: a Boolean for the bit \verb|RATPOINTS_NO_REVERSE| of the flags
    that \verb|main.c| clears, whose Jacobi switch is that of the sieving parameters and whose output
    bits are stage~G's, and an integer for \verb|args->sturm|; the reversal check runs whenever the
    options allow it, without \verb|RATPOINTS_NO_REVERSE_AUTO| (stage~H);
  \item a list of integers for the array of GMP integers of the coefficients, a new list for the
    reversal in place, and nothing written back to the caller (the reversed coefficients, the
    degree, the flags), so that there is no state between calls for the reset of the flags to clear;
  \item the rows of the chain as arrays of their coefficients up to their degree, for the table of
    $(d + 1)^2$ GMP integers and the array of degrees, with no \verb|mpz_init| and \verb|mpz_clear|;
  \item of the Sturm step only its test for a repeated factor, the rest being stage~H;
  \item of \verb|examine_prime| what the table, its last entry and the exit need: the coefficients
    modulo $p$ that it hands back serve the forbidden divisors (stage~E), and its entry of the prime
    and its codes $0$, $1$ and $2$ the choice of the moduli;
  \item the primes of the exit are the moduli of the parameters, where ratpoints examines the first
    \verb|num_primes| primes of the table and more when the number was left to it; when the exit
    fires there are no points, so the output is the same; the arguments of \verb|sieving_info| that
    serve the choice of the moduli (the list of the moduli, the mean number of bits per word, whether
    the number of primes was left to the library) and the 2-adic classes (stage~E) are not modelled;
  \item no 2-adic exit (stage~E), which ends only searches without points;
  \item integers for the \verb|long|s, with the range obligations of the helper \verb|mod| and of the
    negations of the map stated as lemmas; no failure for lack of memory; no output under
    \verb|RATPOINTS_VERBOSE| or \verb|DEBUG|, and not the line of the report without \verb|-q| that says
    that the polynomial was reversed (\csource{main.c}{1005}{1006}).
\end{itemize}
The lines of the set-up that are neither modelled nor left to a later stage are excluded in
\verb|correspondence/ratpoints.toml|: the output under \verb|RATPOINTS_VERBOSE| and \verb|DEBUG|, the
management of memory and its failures, the checks of arguments that only a caller of the library can fail, and
the threads; so the record of \verb|find_points_work_1| up to the run lacks only what belongs to
stages~E, F, H and~I. The macros of the flags and of the return codes in \verb|ratpoints.h| are
recorded later, for all stages together.
