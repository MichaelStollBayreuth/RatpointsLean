\chapter{The tables and the moduli}\label{chap:tables}

Stage~F models the side of the numerators: the tables of the moduli, the three stages of the sieve,
the packing of the numerators of a class by its stride, and the first stage in chunks of bit arrays
with the rows as the builders of ratpoints lay them out. It runs in four sub-stages with a chapter
each: F1, the tables and the moduli (this chapter); F2, the stages of the sieve and the check
(Chapter~\ref{chap:stages}); F3, the packing (Chapter~\ref{chap:packing}); F4, the chunked first
stage and the faithful rows (Chapter~\ref{chap:rows}). Each of them only computes what the model
computes already in another way, or removes candidates that are not points, so the points found and
their order do not change.

F1 models what ratpoints does with its tables before the sieve. The table of a prime is computed as
\verb|examine_prime| computes it, by Horner's rule in the arithmetic of 64-bit words with the
Barrett reduction on a fixed schedule, and it hands back to its caller the coefficients modulo the
prime and its entry at infinity; the forbidden divisors of Chapter~\ref{chap:denominators} take them
from it, prime by prime in the loop over the primes that \verb|sieving_info| examines, which ends
the search at a prime modulo which the curve has no points. And the moduli may be prime powers and
products of prime powers, as those of ratpoints are: their tables are those of \verb|examine_power|,
and their rows are the rows of their factors, combined.

In this sub-stage the theorems of Chapter~\ref{chap:denominators}, the refusal theorems and the
statements that comparator certifies keep their text, and change only through the parameters that
they quantify over (Definition~\ref{def:Params}): the parameters have the number of primes that the
loop of the tables examines, and their well-formedness admits composite moduli, below
$2^{\mathrm{maxBitsInPrime}}$ as in the C code, where before it admitted the $171$ primes of the
table; it does not admit the primes of the table from $2^{\mathrm{maxBitsInPrime}}$ on. The bound is
the C code's own: ratpoints examines only the primes below $2^{\mathrm{maxBitsInPrime}}$, and its
schedule of the Barrett reduction is exact for them (and, at the $8$ bits of the build, for no
larger prime from degree $7$ on). The parameters of the command line are well-formed as before, and
the output is the same for every well-formed choice; since stage~I the program gives the options of
the sieve (Definition~\ref{def:sieveOptions}), from which the search chooses its moduli itself,
composite moduli among them, as ratpoints does (Chapter~\ref{chap:moduli}), and the theorems hold
for every choice that it makes.

\section{What the C code does with its tables}\label{sec:tablesC}

For the polynomial searched, of degree $d$ with the coefficients $c_0, \dots, c_d$ and the form
$F(x, z) = \sum_k c_k x^k z^{D - k}$ of even degree $D = d + (d \bmod 2)$, \verb|sieving_info|:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item examines the first \verb|pn_lim| primes of the table in turn, \verb|pn_lim| being at first
    the number of primes asked for, \verb|num_primes|, at most $\mathrm{numPrimes}$, the primes
    below $2^{\mathrm{maxBitsInPrime}}$, and raised when the number was left to the library and the
    choice of the moduli needs more primes (\csource{find_points.c}{2605}{2707}).
    \verb|examine_prime| (\csource{find_points.c}{1683}{1826}) reduces the coefficients modulo $p$,
    by \verb|mod| for \verb|long|s and by the Euclidean remainder for GMP integers, into an array of
    its caller; sets the entry $0$ of the table from the squares modulo $p$ at $c_0$, and the entry
    $a$ for $0 < a < p$ from $f(a)$, computed by Horner's rule in \verb|unsigned long|s with the
    Barrett reduction, once at the end for $d$ at most
    $\mathrm{RP\_HORNER\_STEPS} = 64 / \mathrm{maxBitsInPrime} - 1$, and also after every
    $\mathrm{RP\_HORNER\_STEPS}$ steps otherwise (\csource{find_points.c}{1628}{1655},
    \csource{find_points.c}{1716}{1747}); sets the entry $p$, whether there is a point at infinity
    modulo $p$; and returns $-1$, the exit, when no entry is set, $0$ when every residue gives a
    square and there is no point at infinity, and otherwise makes the sieve entry of the prime. It
    hands back the table and the coefficients;
  \item for each examined prime, in the same loop and whatever \verb|examine_prime| returned short
    of the exit, at which \verb|sieving_info| returns at once, while the checked loop over the
    denominators is on and fewer than \verb|max_forbidden| forbidden divisors have been found,
    records the forbidden divisor of $p$ from the coefficients modulo $p$ and the entry at infinity
    that it handed back (\csource{find_points.c}{2624}{2677}); after the loop, the further primes of
    the table from the final position \verb|pn_lim| on (\csource{find_points.c}{2709}{2789});
  \item offers as moduli the odd composites $m$ with $9 \le m < 2^{\mathrm{maxBitsInPrime}}$ and
    $m \le \mathrm{RATPOINTS\_COMPOSITE\_MAX}$, $64$ by default, that factor over the examined
    primes into at most three prime powers, each of which says something about the curve, and whose
    table is not too dear for the run, while there is room for its prime powers and for more
    candidates (\verb|add_moduli|, \csource{find_points.c}{2339}{2411}); for a factor $p^e$ with
    $e \ge 2$, \verb|examine_power| (\csource{find_points.c}{2254}{2331}) computes the coefficients
    modulo $q = p^e$, the squares modulo $q$, for each residue $x$ whether $f(x)$ is a square modulo
    $q$, for each multiple $t$ of $p$ whether the reversed polynomial
    $\tilde f(t) = \sum_k c_k t^{D - k}$ is one, and the inverses modulo $q$ of the units, by the
    extended Euclidean algorithm (\verb|modinv|, \csource{find_points.c}{2241}{2252});
  \item ranks the primes and the composites by what they say per cost and takes the moduli of the
    first stage in that order, then ranks the rest again with a key for the second stage and takes
    its moduli, pairwise coprime throughout, and makes the sieve entry of each composite
    (\verb|make_power| and \verb|make_modulus|, \csource{find_points.c}{2414}{2475}); the third
    stage takes primes only, and examines further primes of the table when it needs them and the
    number of primes was left to the library, with the same exit
    (\csource{find_points.c}{2976}{3000}).
\end{enumerate}
The row of a denominator $b$ in the table of a modulus is then, for each kind, a pattern of the
numerators modulo the modulus: for a prime $p$ and $r = b \bmod p \ne 0$, the residues $x$ with
$f(x r^{-1})$ a square modulo $p$, and for $r = 0$ those not divisible by $p$; for a prime power
$q = p^e$ and $r = b \bmod q$, the residues $x$ with $f(x r^{-1})$ a square modulo $q$ when
$p \nmid r$, and with $p \nmid x$ and $\tilde f(r x^{-1})$ a square modulo $q$ when $p \mid r$
(\verb|_ratpoints_sieve_init_power|, \csource{init.c}{359}{393}); for a product, the residues that
the row of every factor admits at its own residue (\verb|_ratpoints_sieve_init_product|,
\csource{init.c}{399}{445}). Every point $(a, y, b)$ with $\gcd(a, b) = 1$ passes every row: for
$p \nmid b$, $F(a, b) \equiv b^D f(a b^{-1})$ modulo $q$ with $b^D$ the square of a unit; for
$p \mid b$, $p \nmid a$ and $F(a, b) \equiv a^D \tilde f(b a^{-1})$ modulo $q$. Which moduli are
chosen, of which kinds and in which stages, changes neither the points nor their order.

\section{The table of a prime as built}\label{sec:tableBuilt}

\begin{definition}[The Barrett reduction on the schedule of Horner's rule]\label{def:barrett}
  \uses{def:primeTable}
  \lean{Ratpoints.maxPrimeEven, Ratpoints.hornerSteps, Ratpoints.one_le_hornerSteps, Ratpoints.maxBitsInPrime_le_ten, Ratpoints.maxPrimeEven_le_two_pow_10, Ratpoints.maxPrimeEven_le_two_pow_32, Ratpoints.getElem!_primeTable_lt_maxPrimeEven, List.SortedLE.getElem_lt_of_lt_length_filter, Ratpoints.Sieve.barrett, Ratpoints.Sieve.hornerBarrett}
  \leanok
  The power of two above the moduli, $\mathrm{maxPrimeEven} = 2^{\mathrm{maxBitsInPrime}}$
  (\csource{rp-private.h}{325}{361}), and the number of Horner steps between two reductions,
  $\mathrm{hornerSteps} = 64 / \mathrm{maxBitsInPrime} - 1$ (\csource{find_points.c}{1652}{1655}),
  both computed from the constant $\mathrm{maxBitsInPrime}$; two facts about the constant, checked
  by evaluation as the C code fixes them at compile time: $1 \le \mathrm{hornerSteps}$, which the
  schedule needs, and $\mathrm{maxBitsInPrime} \le 10$, since the header knows only the sizes from
  $5$ to $10$ bits and takes $7$ for any other, so that the moduli are below $2^{10}$, as the
  comments of \verb|examine_power| say, and below $2^{32}$; and the first $\mathrm{numPrimes}$
  primes of the table are below $\mathrm{maxPrimeEven}$, since the table is sorted. The Barrett
  reduction of $u$ modulo $p$ with the reciprocal $m = \lfloor (2^{64} - 1) / p \rfloor$:
  $r = u - \lfloor u m / 2^{64} \rfloor p$, and $r - p$ if $r \ge p$
  (\csource{find_points.c}{1639}{1650}, the form with a 128-bit product, which the default build and
  the build of the model compile). The value of $f$ at $a$ modulo $p$ from the coefficients modulo
  $p$ by Horner's rule from $c_d$, without a reduction for $d \le \mathrm{hornerSteps}$, and with a
  reduction after every $\mathrm{hornerSteps}$ steps otherwise, and one at the end
  (\csource{find_points.c}{1724}{1724}, \csource{find_points.c}{1726}{1731},
  \csource{find_points.c}{1735}{1735}, \csource{find_points.c}{1737}{1744}).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The Barrett reduction and the schedule of Horner's rule]\label{lem:barrett}
  \uses{def:barrett, def:examinePrime}
  \lean{Ratpoints.Sieve.barrett_eq, Ratpoints.hornerSteps_add_one, Ratpoints.pow_hornerSteps_add_one_lt, Ratpoints.Sieve.HornerInvariant, Ratpoints.Sieve.HornerInvariant.lt_two_pow_64, Ratpoints.Sieve.hornerBarrett_spec, Nat.mul_sub_one_div_div_mul_le, Nat.lt_mul_sub_one_div_div_add_two_mul}
  \leanok
  For $0 < p$ and $u < 2^{64}$ the Barrett reduction gives $u \bmod p$. For
  $p < \mathrm{maxPrimeEven}$, coefficients below $p$ and $a < p$, the accumulator of Horner's rule
  stays below $p^{\mathrm{hornerSteps} + 1} < 2^{64}$, so that the schedule gives $f(a) \bmod p$,
  in both of its branches.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Write $2^{64} - 1 = m p + t$ with $t < p$. Then $u/p - u m/2^{64} = u (1 + t)/(p\, 2^{64})$ lies
  in $[0, 1)$, so the quotient $\lfloor u m / 2^{64} \rfloor$ is $\lfloor u / p \rfloor$ or one
  less, and one subtraction of $p$ ends in $[0, p)$. After a reduction the accumulator is below $p$,
  and each step $s \mapsto s a + c$ with $a, c < p$ takes a bound $p^j$ to $p^{j + 1}$; a reduction
  follows at the latest after $\mathrm{hornerSteps}$ steps, so the accumulator stays below
  $p^{64 / \mathrm{maxBitsInPrime}}$, which is less than
  $2^{\mathrm{maxBitsInPrime} \cdot (64 / \mathrm{maxBitsInPrime})} \le 2^{64}$, whatever the
  degree. This is the range obligation of the accumulator, which in the C code is an
  \verb|unsigned long|; the invariant of the loop holds the residue of the accumulator and this
  bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The table with the exit is the table]\label{lem:examinePrime_eq}
  \uses{lem:barrett, def:examinePrimeExit, def:examinePrime}
  \lean{Ratpoints.Sieve.ExaminePrimeInvariant, Array.any_push_id_eq_false_iff, Ratpoints.Sieve.examinePrime?_spec, Ratpoints.Sieve.run_examinePrime?, Ratpoints.Sieve.eq_examinePrime_of_run_examinePrime?_eq_some, Ratpoints.Sieve.eq_examinePrime_of_lt_numPrimes_of_run_examinePrime?_eq_some, Ratpoints.Sieve.eq_map_range_of_run_examinePrime?_eq_some, Ratpoints.Sieve.run_examinePrime?_eq_none_iff}
  \leanok
  The table of a prime with the exit (Definition~\ref{def:examinePrimeExit}) is a component of type
  \verb|Id| with the loop of \verb|examine_prime| over the residues: the entry $0$ from the squares
  modulo $p$ at $c_0$, the entries $1, \dots, p - 1$ by the schedule of
  Definition~\ref{def:barrett}, and the entry $p$, whether there is a point at infinity modulo $p$;
  it keeps $p + 1$ entries, as \verb|examine_prime| does, and hands back the coefficients modulo $p$
  with the table. The reference (Definition~\ref{def:examinePrime}) has the entry at infinity too:
  for even $d$ whether $c_d$ is a square modulo $p$, and true for odd $d$. For
  $0 < p < \mathrm{maxPrimeEven}$, in particular for the primes of the table at the positions below
  $\mathrm{numPrimes}$: the table with the exit is none exactly when no entry of the reference is
  true, and otherwise it is the reference, with the coefficients reduced modulo $p$, beside the code
  and the density, which no contract reads; the count of the residues with a square value is the
  count of the true entries of the table, so that the exit, which tests the count and the entry at
  infinity, is the test whether one entry is true.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Lemma~\ref{lem:barrett} for each entry $1, \dots, p - 1$, in an invariant of the loop over the
  residues, which holds the count of the true entries too; the entry $0$ and the entry at infinity
  read the same squares in both.
\end{proof}

The lemmas on the rows and the exit (Lemmas~\ref{lem:admits_examinePrime}
and~\ref{lem:examinePrime_none}) are those of the table of a prime, the exit with the hypothesis
$p < \mathrm{maxPrimeEven}$, which every prime that the model examines satisfies, and with the lemma
that a point makes some entry of the reference true.

\section{Prime powers}\label{sec:powers}

\begin{definition}[The inverse modulo $m$ by the extended Euclidean algorithm]\label{def:modinv}
  \lean{Ratpoints.Sieve.modinv}
  \leanok
  From $r_0 = m$, $r_1 = x \bmod m$ and the cofactors $s_0 = 0$, $s_1 = 1$, which keep
  $r_i \equiv s_i x \pmod m$: while $r_1 \ne 0$, with $q = \lfloor r_0 / r_1 \rfloor$, the pair
  $(r_0, r_1)$ becomes $(r_1, r_0 - q r_1)$ and $(s_0, s_1)$ becomes $(s_1, s_0 - q s_1)$; then
  $s_0$, plus $m$ if it is negative (\csource{find_points.c}{2241}{2252}).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The inverse modulo $m$]\label{lem:modinv}
  \uses{def:modinv}
  \lean{Ratpoints.Sieve.ModinvInvariant, Ratpoints.Sieve.modinv_spec, Ratpoints.Sieve.ModinvInvariant.natAbs_add_mul_le, Int.natAbs_sub_mul_of_mul_nonpos}
  \leanok
  For $1 < m$ and $x$ prime to $m$, the result is the inverse of $x$ modulo $m$ in $[0, m)$; the
  cofactors and the products
  $q s_1$ stay in $[-m, m]$, which is their range obligation.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The invariant: the two congruences, $\gcd(r_0, r_1) = \gcd(m, x)$, $r_0 > 0$, cofactors of
  opposite signs with $|s_0| r_1 + |s_1| r_0 = m$, and $|s_0| \le m$; the loop ends since $r_1$
  decreases. At the end $r_0 = \gcd(m, x) = 1$, so $s_0 x \equiv 1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[The tables of a prime power]\label{def:examinePower}
  \uses{def:modinv, def:useCLong, def:examinePrime, def:powerWords, def:densities}
  \lean{Ratpoints.Sieve.Power, Ratpoints.Sieve.examinePower, Ratpoints.Sieve.Power.admits, Ratpoints.Sieve.Power.useful}
  \leanok
  For a prime $p$ and $e \ge 2$, $q = p^e$, and whether denominators divisible by $p$ occur, a
  component of type \verb|Id|: the coefficients reduced modulo $q$ on the path of the flag of the
  \verb|long|s, with $c_D = 0$ for odd $d$; the bit arrays \verb|sq|, \verb|fsq| and \verb|gsq|
  of $\mathrm{modWords}$ words cleared in one loop, and the bits of the squares modulo $q$ set in
  \verb|sq| (Definition~\ref{def:powerWords}); for each residue $x$, bit $x$ of \verb|fsq| set
  when $f(x)$ is a square modulo $q$, by Horner's rule reduced at every step, these residues
  counted; for each multiple $t < q$ of $p$, bit $t$ of \verb|gsq| set when $\tilde f(t)$ is a
  square modulo $q$, the squares read from \verb|sq| (Definition~\ref{def:densities}); in a loop
  over the residues, the inverses of the units modulo $q$ by Definition~\ref{def:modinv}
  (Definition~\ref{def:powerWords}); and the density of the prime power from the count and the
  switch (Definition~\ref{def:densities}; \csource{find_points.c}{2267}{2282},
  \csource{find_points.c}{2285}{2293}, \csource{find_points.c}{2301}{2301},
  \csource{find_points.c}{2331}{2331}; \csource{rp-private.h}{531}{534}). The prime power is
  \emph{useful}, the code $1$ that \verb|examine_power| returns, when its density is below
  $1 - 10^{-9}$ (\csource{find_points.c}{2330}{2330}). The row of $b$ admits $a$
  when, for $r = b \bmod q$ and $x = a \bmod q$, bit $x r^{-1}$ of \verb|fsq| is set, that is
  $f(x r^{-1})$ is a square modulo $q$, if $p \nmid r$, and $p \nmid x$ and bit $r x^{-1}$ of
  \verb|gsq| is set, that is $\tilde f(r x^{-1})$ is a square modulo $q$, if $p \mid r$: the
  admission test, which the builder of the rows of a prime power reads off the tables
  (Definition~\ref{def:sieveInitPower}).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The rows of a prime power let every point through]\label{lem:power_admits}
  \uses{def:examinePower, lem:modinv, lem:isSquare_div, lem:twoAdic_point, def:examinePrime,
    def:barrett, def:binaryForm, lem:powerWords}
  \lean{Ratpoints.Sieve.examinePower_spec, Ratpoints.Sieve.examinePower_eq_examinePower_false, Ratpoints.Sieve.bitAt_fsq_run_examinePower_of_isSquare, Ratpoints.Sieve.bitAt_gsq_run_examinePower_of_isSquare, Ratpoints.Sieve.mul_getElem!_inv_run_examinePower_mod, Ratpoints.Sieve.natCast_evalMod_reverse, Ratpoints.Sieve.admits_run_examinePower_of_sq_eq, Ratpoints.mul_add_lt_two_pow_64_of_lt_maxPrimeEven, Ratpoints.mul_self_lt_two_pow_63_of_lt_maxPrimeEven, Ratpoints.Sieve.getElem!_inv_run_examinePower_lt, Ratpoints.Sieve.getElem!_inv_run_examinePower_lt_two_pow_16}
  \leanok
  The two paths of the coefficients give the same tables; for $q \le 64\,\mathrm{modWords}$, within
  the words of the bit arrays, and whether or not denominators divisible by $p$ occur, which only
  the density reads, the bits of the tables mark what they claim: $f(x)$ a square modulo $q$,
  $\tilde f(t)$ a square modulo $q$ for $p \mid t$, and, by the contract of the loop of the
  inverses, the inverses of the units. For a prime $p$, $e \ge 1$ with
  $p^e \le 64\,\mathrm{modWords}$, $\gcd(a, b) = 1$ and $y^2 = F(a, b)$, the row of $b$ in the
  tables of $p^e$ admits $a$. The values of \verb|examine_power| and of the row stay in their C
  types, the moduli being below $\mathrm{maxPrimeEven} \le 2^{10}$: the accumulator $v x + c$ of
  Horner's rule with $v, x, c < q$, an \verb|unsigned long|, and likewise the product of a residue
  and an inverse in the row; the square $x \cdot x$, a signed \verb|long|; and the inverses, below
  $q$, \verb|unsigned short|s.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \uses{lem:modinv, lem:powerWords}
  \leanok
  The bits of the tables come from the contracts of the loops that set them
  (Lemma~\ref{lem:powerWords}). For $p \nmid b$, $b$ is a unit modulo $q$ and
  $F(a, b) = b^D f(a b^{-1})$ there, with $b^D$ the square of a unit (Lemma~\ref{lem:isSquare_div}).
  For $p \mid b$, $p \nmid a$, so $a$ is a unit, and $F(a, b) = a^D \tilde f(b a^{-1})$ modulo $q$,
  by the form of that lemma with the roles of $a$ and $b$ exchanged (Lemma~\ref{lem:twoAdic_point});
  Horner's rule on the reversed coefficients computes $\tilde f$. Zero counts as a square, so a high
  power of $p$ dividing $F(a, b)$ needs no case of its own.
\end{proof}

The coefficients reduced by \verb|mod| need moduli below $2^{59}$ (Definition~\ref{def:useCLong}),
which $\mathrm{maxPrimeEven} \le 2^{32}$ gives.

\section{The sieve entry of a modulus}\label{sec:sieveEntry}

\begin{definition}[The sieve entries]\label{def:SieveEntry}
  \uses{def:admits, def:examinePower, def:binaryForm, def:entryRef, def:rowInit}
  \lean{Ratpoints.Sieve.Factor, Ratpoints.Sieve.Factor.admits, Ratpoints.Sieve.Factor.modulus, Ratpoints.Sieve.Factor.ref, Ratpoints.Sieve.Factor.init, Ratpoints.Sieve.SieveEntry, Ratpoints.Sieve.SieveEntry.refInit, Ratpoints.Sieve.SieveEntry.admits, Ratpoints.Sieve.survives, Ratpoints.Sieve.SieveEntry.IsSound}
  \leanok
  A factor of a modulus is a prime with its table or a prime power with its tables, its modulus the
  prime or the prime power; the sieve entry of a modulus $m$ is $m$ with its factors, one for a
  prime or a prime power, two or more for a product, the reciprocal of $m$
  (Definition~\ref{def:mulMod}), and the fields that the packing of the numerators reads
  (Chapter~\ref{chap:packing}): the bias, the least multiple of $m$ from $\mathrm{rowBias}$ on, the
  inverse $\mathrm{rbainv}$ of $\mathrm{rbaLength}$ modulo $m$, and the inverses $\mathrm{dinv}$ of
  the strides $2^k$ modulo $m$ up to the largest stride in use, which the rows of offsets fill
  (Definition~\ref{def:classOffsets}); the reference to the entry whose table of rows holds its rows
  and the kind of its builder (Definitions~\ref{def:entryRef} and~\ref{def:rowInit}); and its
  density, a \verb|Float|, and the count of its factors, $0$ for a prime, $1$ for a prime power and
  their number for a product, which the correction of the moduli reads
  (Definition~\ref{def:correction}) (\csource{rp-private.h}{542}{555}). The reference of a factor,
  to which the field \verb|factor| of the entry of a product points, is that of its prime or of its
  prime power; the kind of the builder of the entry of a factor is, for a prime, the kind of the
  builder of the prime of its table, which \verb|examine_prime| sets (line 1800), and for a prime
  power the builder of a prime power, which \verb|make_power| sets (line 2420); and the reference
  and the kind of the builder of the modulus at the position $n$ with its factors are, for one
  factor (a prime or a prime power), those of the entry of that factor, whose entry the modulus
  shares, as \verb|sieve_list[n]| is \verb|prime_se[pn]| or \verb|power_se[idx]|, and for a product
  the reference to its own entry, by the position $n$, and the builder of a product. Its row of $b$
  admits $a$ when the row of every factor admits $a$, each factor reading $b$ and $a$ modulo its own
  modulus, which divides $m$, the admission test, which the builder of the rows of a product reads
  off the rows of its factors (Definition~\ref{def:sieveInitProduct}); a numerator \emph{survives} a
  list of sieve entries for $b$ when the row of $b$ of every entry admits it, the test that
  Definition~\ref{def:admits} gave for the tables of primes. A sieve entry is \emph{sound} when its
  modulus is positive and its row of $b$ admits $a$ for every $\gcd(a, b) = 1$ and $y^2 = F(a, b)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The rows of a modulus let every point through]\label{lem:sieveEntry_admits}
  \uses{def:SieveEntry, def:examinePrime, def:squaresMod, def:mulMod, def:packingConstants,
    lem:mulMod}
  \lean{Ratpoints.Sieve.Factor.IsSound, Ratpoints.Sieve.Factor.IsSound.inverses_eq, Ratpoints.Sieve.mod_sixty_four_ne_zero_of_prime, Ratpoints.IsModulusEntry, Ratpoints.IsModulusEntry.isSound, Ratpoints.IsModulusEntry.factors_ne_nil, Ratpoints.IsModulusEntry.p_eq_modulus, Ratpoints.mod_two_eq_one_of_forall_dvd, Ratpoints.coprime_rbaLength_of_mod_two_eq_one}
  \leanok

  every $\gcd(a, b) = 1$ with $y^2 = F(a, b)$, its modulus divides $m$ and its table of inverses
  inverts its units (Lemma~\ref{lem:admits_mul}), its builder has what it needs
  (Lemma~\ref{lem:fetchRow}), and its tables are those of the reference, those of
  \verb|examinePrime| and of \verb|examinePower| on the path of GMP integers, the latter with some
  value of the switch whether denominators divisible by $p$ occur; then a prime factor has the
  inverses of Definition~\ref{def:squaresMod}, and a prime is no multiple of $64$. An entry of $m$
  as the choice of the moduli makes it has the modulus $m$, a row of $b$ that admits $a$ for every
  $\gcd(a, b) = 1$ with $y^2 = F(a, b)$, factors whose moduli multiply to $m$, with their tables of
  inverses for $m$, builders that have what they need and the tables of the reference, and it is
  \emph{packable} (Lemma~\ref{lem:classOffsets}): $m$ is odd, the entry holds the reciprocal of $m$,
  $\mathrm{rbainv} < m$ with $\mathrm{rbainv} \cdot \mathrm{rbaLength} \equiv 1 \pmod m$, and the
  bias is a multiple of $m$ in $[\mathrm{rowBias}, \mathrm{rowBias} + m)$. Such an entry is sound
  (Definition~\ref{def:SieveEntry}), it has a factor, and a single factor has the modulus $m$. A
  modulus each of whose divisors $q > 1$ is divisible by one of the first $\mathrm{numPrimes}$
  primes of the table is odd, the table holding no $2$, and an odd modulus is prime to
  $\mathrm{rbaLength} = 2^{\mathrm{rbaShift}}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \uses{lem:classOffsets}
  \leanok
  The row of the entry admits $a$ when the row of every factor does, each factor reading $b$ and
  $a$ modulo $m$, which its sound row admits; no Chinese remainder theorem is needed: the entry
  only intersects the conditions of its factors. The moduli of the factors multiply to $m > 1$, so
  there is a factor, and a single one has the modulus $m$. The inverses of a prime factor are those
  of its reference table. An odd prime is no multiple of $64$; the least prime of the table is $3$,
  so no prime of the table divides $2$, and $\mathrm{rbaLength}$ is a power of $2$.
\end{proof}

The rows of a sieve entry that the run reads are built by the builders of ratpoints, the functions
of \verb|init.c| for each prime, \verb|lay_out_pattern| for the prime powers and the blocks of
\verb|_ratpoints_sieve_init_product|, which are in Chapter~\ref{chap:rows}, proved to build the
closed forms of the admission tests; the bits of a row depend on the entry only through the test,
which reduces $b$ and $a$ modulo $m$ first, so the words of Chapter~\ref{chap:words} and their
contracts hold for the sieve entries of every kind with the proofs that they had for the tables of
primes.

\section{The parameters and the search}\label{sec:tablesSearch}

The parameters (Definition~\ref{def:Params}) have the number of primes that the loop of the tables
examines, from which the forbidden divisors of the further primes start; their moduli may be primes
of the table, powers of them and products of such powers. In this sub-stage they were well-formed
when every modulus $m$ had $1 < m < \mathrm{maxPrimeEven}$ and every divisor $q > 1$ of $m$ was
divisible by one of the first $\mathrm{numPrimes}$ primes of the table, the moduli of the third
stage were primes of the table, at most $\mathrm{numPrimes}$ primes were examined and the cut points
were in order within the moduli; since stage~I they are the result of the choice of the moduli that
the search makes from the curve (Definition~\ref{def:choice}), as \verb|sieving_info| makes it: the
primes of the table that say something about the curve, the composite moduli that \verb|add_moduli|
offers and \verb|make_modulus| makes (Definition~\ref{def:compositeModuli}), all of them odd and
below $\mathrm{maxPrimeEven}$, and in the third stage and the reserve primes of the table. The
soundness of the sieve entries needs none of the rules by which the choice takes its moduli
(pairwise coprimality, the bound of three factors, the bound $\mathrm{RATPOINTS\_COMPOSITE\_MAX}$,
the cost of the table, what a modulus says about the curve, the number of candidates): it needs only
that the factors of an entry are sound for its modulus and multiply to it
(Lemma~\ref{lem:sieveEntry_admits}), which the contract of the choice states of every entry that it
makes, with the reciprocals of the entries, the forbidden divisors of the two loops and the store of
the tables of rows that fits all the entries (Lemma~\ref{lem:choice}); when the choice takes an
exit, the curve has no points of any height, having none modulo a prime that it examined.

The search (Definition~\ref{def:searchPoints}) takes its sieve entries and its forbidden divisors
from the choice of the moduli, and otherwise runs as in Chapter~\ref{chap:denominators}; the
soundness of the run over a shape takes sound sieve entries. In this sub-stage its theorem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:findPoints_order}), the main theorems and the refusal theorems keep their text:
the output is the list of the points at infinity followed by the points of the brute-force reference
over the denominators of the shape in its order, each mapped back after a reversal, for every
well-formed choice of the parameters, and since stage~I for every choice of the moduli that the
search makes, also when an exit is taken.

\section{The command-line program and the differential tests}\label{sec:tablesCli}

The program is that of stage~E: its parameters examine the first $q$ primes and take primes as
moduli. F1 makes no further invocation of the test scripts comparable, since the options that it
concerns have been read since stage~C1 and the output does not change; the differential tests stay
as they are, every point list compared in its order. Composite moduli, which ratpoints chooses from
the curve, the program chooses too since stage~I, as ratpoints does
(Definition~\ref{def:compositeModuli}); \verb|#guard| examples run the search with composite moduli
in the stages, made as the choice makes them, and compare the output with the brute-force reference,
and with the reference of the reversed polynomial mapped back when the search reverses.

\section{Deviations}\label{sec:tablesDeviations}

Each component of this chapter differs from the C code in a stated way, recorded in its
\verb|Deviation:| paragraph:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the arithmetic of \verb|unsigned long|s in the Barrett reduction and in Horner's rule is
    that of natural numbers, with the bound $2^{64}$ of the accumulator a lemma
    (Lemma~\ref{lem:barrett}); the branch of the schedule is chosen for each residue, where
    \verb|examine_prime| chooses it once; natural numbers for the remainders of \verb|modinv|, whose
    correction of a negative $x \bmod m$ then has no counterpart, and for the \verb|long|s of
    \verb|examine_power| and of the rows of prime powers, integers for the cofactors of
    \verb|modinv|, with their bounds lemmas (Lemmas~\ref{lem:modinv} and~\ref{lem:power_admits});
  \item \verb|examine_prime| returns its codes, its density and its entry together, as natural
    numbers and a \verb|Float|. Its code $0$, every residue a square and no point at infinity, makes
    no sieve entry in ratpoints and a table in the model, which the choice of the moduli leaves out
    of its pool, as ratpoints does (Definition~\ref{def:tablesLoop}). The fields of the sieve entry
    of a prime, the reciprocal, the bias and the inverse of $\mathrm{rbaLength}$ among them, are
    those of a sieve entry with the prime as its single factor, set where the choice of the moduli
    makes the entry of the prime, with its density (Definition~\ref{def:compositeModuli});
  \item the coefficients and the tables are fresh arrays, where the C code fills arrays of its
    caller and buffers; the records of the prime powers that \verb|add_moduli| examines are kept in
    a table passed on, and the sieve entry of a prime power is made for each modulus that needs it,
    where \verb|make_modulus| makes it once and keeps it by its index, whose table of rows the model
    makes once, by its prime and its exponent (Definition~\ref{def:storeTables}); the test of room
    in the buffer of the prime powers, which never fails, is not made
    (Definition~\ref{def:compositeModuli});
  \item the rows of a prime power and of a product are built by the builders of
    Chapter~\ref{chap:rows}, with their deviations (Section~\ref{sec:rowsDeviations}): the rows of
    the factors of a product read plainly, for the acquire load, and every row stored plainly, for
    the release store, both for the threads, which the model does not have;
  \item the search makes the choice of the moduli, with its ranking, its composite moduli and its
    stages (Section~\ref{sec:choice}), whose deviations its components state;
  \item the reciprocal, the bias and the inverse of $\mathrm{rbaLength}$ of a sieve entry are set
    with the entry for every kind of modulus, with the formulas of the C code
    (Definition~\ref{def:compositeModuli}), the inverse of a prime read from its table; the inverses
    \verb|dinv| an array that the rows of offsets fill (Definition~\ref{def:classOffsets}).
\end{itemize}
The lines of this part of the C code that are neither modelled nor left to a later stage are
excluded in \verb|correspondence/ratpoints.toml|: the check \verb|RP_VERIFY_MODULI| of the rows of
the moduli against the form, compiled only on request (\verb|init.c|); the output of
\verb|sieving_info| under \verb|RP_PRIME_STATS| and \verb|DEBUG| between statements that are not
modelled, where the output inside a modelled block goes with the block, whose deviation says so, its
report under \verb|RATPOINTS_VERBOSE| being that of Definition~\ref{def:report}; and the enlargement
of the buffer of the tables for every prime looked at, those of the third stage among them, and for
the composite moduli of the first two stages with their prime-power factors, which fails only for
lack of memory. The reciprocals of the moduli are fields of their sieve entries, which
\verb|sieving_info| collects at its end (\csource{find_points.c}{3064}{3070};
Definition~\ref{def:choice}); the third stage reads them (Definition~\ref{def:stage3}), and so do
the first two stages, stepping them with the sieve specs (Definitions~\ref{def:chunkStarts}
or~\ref{def:andRows}, by the arm, and~\ref{def:secondStage}); the search makes the choice of the
moduli, with the densities of the primes and the powers, \verb|add_moduli|, the stages, the ranking
and the rule for looking at further primes (Section~\ref{sec:choice}).

\section{The evidence}\label{sec:tables-evidence}

Besides the proofs:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a reading of the C code of the numerator side at the pin, each part with a second reader who
    checked the reading: every part of the numerator side is a data refinement of the model or a
    sound filter, so that the points and their order do not change; the Barrett reduction is exact
    for every $u < 2^{64}$, and the schedule of Horner's rule keeps its accumulator below $2^{64}$
    for the primes below $2^{\mathrm{maxBitsInPrime}}$, at every degree; and two defects of the C
    code without effect on the points, now recorded with ratpoints: \verb|-N| without \verb|-n| is
    not a hard limit, and the check of \verb|RATPOINTS_CHUNK| above $16$ never fires;
  \item before the sub-stage, a sketch of the model in Lean, which agrees with \verb|ratpoints -q|

    in its order; whose tables, coefficients modulo $p$, tables of prime powers and rows are those
