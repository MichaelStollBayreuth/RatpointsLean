\chapter{The stages of the sieve and the check}\label{chap:stages}

F2 models the three stages in which ratpoints applies its moduli to the numerators of a denominator,
and the check of a candidate. The first stage and-s the rows of the first $s_1$ moduli into every
bit array of a piece, as in Chapter~\ref{chap:words}; the second and-s the rows of the moduli from
$s_1$ to $s_2$ into each bit array that the first stage leaves nonzero, one after the other, and
stops when no bit is left; and each numerator whose bit survives goes to the test for common factors
with the denominator, by the binary algorithm of \verb|relprime| on the 64-bit \verb|long|s of the C
code, and then to the third stage, which looks up, for each prime from $s_2$ on, whether the form
can be a square at that numerator, before the exact check. The reductions of the third stage and of
the Jacobi test multiply by the reciprocal of the modulus, which the sieve entries and the tables of
the Jacobi test keep in a field and \verb|sieving_info| collects for the moduli, as in the C code;
the cut points $s_1$ and $s_2$ come from the parameters, and the parameters of the command line take
them by the counting rules of ratpoints.

Each stage only tests the moduli of the parameters, so the numerators that reach the exact check are
those of the unstaged sieve of Chapter~\ref{chap:words} with its check, in the same order. The test
for common factors holds the numerator and the denominator in \verb|long|s, as the C code does,
which is exact for the heights that a \verb|long| holds, the type of the height bound of the
library: so \verb|findPoints| (Definition~\ref{def:findPoints}) refuses a height bound of $2^{63}$
or more with error~4, the error with which the program refuses such a height when it reads it, so
that \verb|ratpoints-lean| answers as the program does; the contracts below \verb|findPoints|
take $H < 2^{63}$. The membership and no-duplicates theorems (Theorem~\ref{thm:findPoints}) keep
their text, which speaks only of the searches that return points; the refusal theorems
(Theorem~\ref{thm:refusals}) gain $2^{63} \le H$ next to $H = 0$; and \verb|wf_sieveParams|
(Definition~\ref{def:sieveParams}) keeps its text, while what it states changes through
\verb|sieveParams|, which the challenge imports from the model: the parameters of the command line
take their three stages by the counting rules of ratpoints. The well-formedness of the parameters
(Definition~\ref{def:Params}) has required the moduli of the third stage to be primes of the table
since stage~F1, which is what the third stage needs. Comparator accepted the seven statements on
2026-10-14, once with the refusal of the heights from $2^{63}$ on and again with the cut points of
this sub-stage, as Chapter~\ref{chap:sieve} and \verb|comparator/README.md| record. The proofs of
the theorems change through the contracts of the run, of \verb|sieving_info| and of the Jacobi test
on which they rest.

\section{What the C code does in the stages}\label{sec:stagesC}

For a denominator $b$, \verb|sift| (\csource{find_points.c}{3442}{3617}) sets the rows of $b$ for
the moduli of the first two stages, clears the flag \verb|stage3_filled| of the sieving thread,
whether the table of the third stage has been filled for $b$, and sets its flag \verb|compute_bc|,
that the products of the check are still to be computed. Then for each piece of its numerators,
\verb|_ratpoints_sift0| (\csource{sift.c}{498}{1305}):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item and-s the rows of the moduli $0, \dots, s_1 - 1$ into every bit array of the piece, from the
    pattern of the 2-adic class, and applies the masks of the ends of the piece;
  \item scans the bit arrays from the first; for each nonzero one with the word number $i$, copies
    it and and-s into the copy the bit array $(i + \mathrm{offset}) \bmod p$ of the row of each
    modulus $p$ from $s_1$ to $s_2 - 1$ in turn, stopping when the copy is zero
    (\csource{sift.c}{1174}{1202}); with \verb|USE_LONG_IN_PHASE_2|, a build option that the default
    build leaves off, it does this one word of the copy at a time (\csource{sift.c}{1113}{1173});
  \item for each set bit of what is left, lowest first, tests the numerator $a$ it stands for:
    \verb|accepted| (\csource{sift.c}{438}{446}) runs \verb|relprime| (\csource{sift.c}{318}{343}),
    the binary algorithm for $\gcd(a, b) = 1$ with a loop body without branches, then fills the
    table of the third stage if it is not filled yet (\verb|fill_checks|,
    \csource{sift.c}{405}{431}) and runs \verb|stage3| (\csource{sift.c}{291}{312}), which for each
    prime $p$ of the third stage that does not divide $b$ rejects $a$ when $f(a b^{-1})$ is not a
    square modulo $p$; a numerator that passes goes to the exact check, which computes the products
    $c_k b^{d - k}$ when the first numerator of $b$ reaches it
    (\csource{find_points.c}{5250}{5338}).
\end{enumerate}
The table of the third stage holds, for each prime $p$: the inverse of $b$ modulo $p$, $0$ when $p
\mid b$, from the residue $b \bmod p$ computed by \verb|RP_MULMOD| for $b < 2^{32}$ and by \verb|%|
above; the reciprocal of $p$; and the bias $p H$ when $2 p H < 2^{32}$, else $0$. With the bias,
\verb|stage3| reduces the value $a b^{-1} + p H$, which lies in $[H, 2 p H - H]$ for $|a| \le H$, by
\verb|RP_MULMOD|; without it, it divides. \verb|RP_MULMOD| and \verb|RP_MULDIV|
(\csource{rp-private.h}{404}{417}) compute the remainder and the quotient of $u$ by $p$ from the
reciprocal $m = \lfloor (2^{64} - 1) / p \rfloor + 1$ with a 128-bit product; the C code relies on
$u < 2^{32}$, and they are exact whenever $u p < 2^{64}$. The Jacobi test by tables
(Definition~\ref{def:jacobiTables}) divides by its primes with \verb|RP_MULDIV|. The reciprocal of a
modulus is a field of its sieve entry, set when the entry is made, and \verb|sieving_info| copies
those of the moduli, and of the further primes that it keeps after them for the correction of the
moduli during the run, in their order, into the array \verb|magics| of its arguments
(\csource{find_points.c}{3064}{3070}), in which \verb|fill_checks| reads the reciprocal of each
prime of the third stage for $b$; the tables of the Jacobi test keep the reciprocals of their
primes. The numerators and the denominators are \verb|long|s throughout: $|a| \le H$ and $b \le H$
for the height bound $H$, itself a \verb|long|.

\verb|sieving_info| chooses the cut points: $s_1$ moduli for the first stage, up to $s_2$ for the
second, and the primes of the third stage up to $s_3$, by the options \verb|-n|, \verb|-N| and
\verb|-P| where they are given and by the cost of each stage, which depends on the curve, where they
are not (\csource{find_points.c}{2803}{3034}). Its third stage takes primes only, and only those
that exclude a residue.

Why the stages change nothing: the and of the rows is commutative and associative, and a bit array
that is zero stays zero, so the second stage leaves exactly the bits that the first stage would
leave with the moduli of both; for a numerator $a$ prime to $b$, the third stage passes $a$ exactly
when the row of $b$ of every prime of the stage admits $a$, since for $p \nmid b$ the row of $b$
reads the same table at the same residue $a b^{-1} \bmod p$ (Definition~\ref{def:admits}), and for
$p \mid b$ the row admits every numerator prime to $p$; and a numerator with a common factor has no
point. So the numerators that reach the exact check are those that survive all the moduli and are
prime to $b$: those that the unstaged sieve keeps and that the test of the $\gcd$ in its check
(Definition~\ref{def:checkNum}) lets through. The stages, like the caches, are a data refinement of
the unstaged sieve with its check: the same numerators reach the exact check, in the same order.

\section{The reductions by a multiplication}\label{sec:mulMod}

\begin{definition}[The reciprocal and the reductions]\label{def:mulMod}
  \uses{def:mulHi}
  \lean{Ratpoints.stage3Limit, Ratpoints.stage3Limit_le_two_pow_32, Ratpoints.Sieve.magic, Ratpoints.Sieve.mulMod, Ratpoints.Sieve.mulDiv}
  \leanok
  The bound $\mathrm{stage3Limit} = 2^{32}$ below which the third stage reduces by a multiplication
  (\verb|RP_STAGE3_LIMIT|, \csource{rp-private.h}{472}{472}), with the fact
  $\mathrm{stage3Limit} \le 2^{32}$ checked by evaluation; the reciprocal of a modulus $p$,
  $\mathrm{magic}(p) = \lfloor (2^{64} - 1) / p \rfloor + 1$, the value that the C code computes
  where it sets the field \verb|magic| of a sieve entry (Definition~\ref{def:SieveEntry}) and of the
  tables of the Jacobi test (Definition~\ref{def:jacobiTables}), and which \verb|sieving_info|
  collects for the moduli in the array \verb|magics| (Definition~\ref{def:sievingInfo}); the
  remainder of $u$ by $p$ from the reciprocal $m$, the low $64$ bits of $m u$, times $p$, shifted
  right by $64$ bits (\csource{rp-private.h}{404}{408}, \csource{rp-private.h}{411}{411},
  \csource{rp-private.h}{413}{414}, \csource{rp-private.h}{417}{417}); and the quotient, the high
  $64$ bits of $u m$ (\csource{rp-private.h}{409}{410}, \csource{rp-private.h}{415}{416}): the
  forms with a 128-bit product, which the default build and the build of the model compile, where
  the others divide. Both compute on $64$-bit words, the arguments cast to words as the macros cast
  them to \verb|unsigned long|, with the C's $64$-bit product of $m$ and $u$ and the high half of
  the $128$-bit product by Definition~\ref{def:mulHi}.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The reductions are exact]\label{lem:mulMod}
  \uses{def:mulMod, lem:mulHi}
  \lean{Ratpoints.Sieve.magic_mul_eq, Ratpoints.Sieve.magic_lt_two_pow_64, Ratpoints.Sieve.mulMod_magic, Ratpoints.Sieve.mulDiv_magic, Ratpoints.Sieve.sub_mulDiv_magic_mul}
  \leanok
  For $0 < p$ and $u p < 2^{64}$, the reduction with the reciprocal of $p$ gives $u \bmod p$; for
  $1 < p$, whose reciprocal is below $2^{64}$, the quotient gives $\lfloor u / p \rfloor$, so that
  $u$ less $p$ times the quotient is $u \bmod p$. The reciprocal $2^{64}$ of $1$ wraps to $0$ on a
  word, as in the C code.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  By Lemma~\ref{lem:mulHi} the words compute the macros on natural numbers, with $m u$ reduced
  modulo $2^{64}$, in which $p$ and $u$ are their own remainders modulo $2^{64}$, since
  $p, u \le u p < 2^{64}$ for $u \ne 0$, and so is $m$ for $1 < p$. Write
  $\mathrm{magic}(p)\, p = 2^{64} + \delta$ with $0 \le \delta < p$ and $u = k p + s$ with $s < p$.
  Then $\mathrm{magic}(p)\, u = k\, 2^{64} + (s\, 2^{64} + u \delta) / p$, whose second part is an
  integer below $2^{64}$, since $s < p$ and $u \delta \le u p < 2^{64}$; so the low half is
  $(s\, 2^{64} + u \delta)/p$ and the high half $k$. The low half times $p$ is
  $s\, 2^{64} + u \delta$, whose high half is $s$.
\end{proof}

The callers satisfy the bound: the denominators that \verb|fill_checks| reduces with the reciprocal
(it takes the remainder of the larger ones) and those of the Jacobi test are at most
$\mathrm{mulModLimit} = 2^{32} - 1$, below $2^{32}$ by a fact checked by evaluation
(\verb|Ratpoints.mulModLimit_lt_two_pow_32|, in Definition~\ref{def:jacobiTables}), and their
moduli, primes of the table, are below $2^{10}$, so that the product is below $2^{64}$
(\verb|Ratpoints.mul_lt_two_pow_64_of_le_mulModLimit|, for moduli up to $2^{32}$); the values that
\verb|stage3| reduces are below $2 p H < \mathrm{stage3Limit} \le 2^{32}$. The Jacobi test by tables
(Definition~\ref{def:jacobiTables}) takes the quotient and the remainder by each of its primes by
the quotient with the reciprocal that its tables keep for the prime, as \verb|jacobi_test| does; its
set-up pushes the reciprocal of each prime with the prime and counts the primes $\equiv 3 \pmod 4$
with an odd exponent in its loop, as \verb|jacobi_setup| does. The contract of the test takes $b q <
2^{64}$ for the primes $q$ of its tables, which the set-up gives as primes of the table with their
reciprocals, and the test of the denominators up to $b_{\mathrm{high}} \le \mathrm{mulModLimit}$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:jacobiTest}) reads that bound off $b \le \mathrm{mulModLimit}$ and $q < 2^{10}$.

\section{The test for common factors}\label{sec:relprime}

\begin{definition}[The binary algorithm of \texttt{relprime}]\label{def:relprime}
  \uses{def:ctz}
  \lean{Ratpoints.Sieve.relprimeOdd, Ratpoints.Sieve.relprime}
  \leanok
  On 64-bit signed words, the \verb|long|s of the C code. For words $m, n$: while $n \ne m$, the
  difference $d = m - n$, the mask $d \gg 63$ by the arithmetic shift, $-1$ for $d < 0$ and $0$
  otherwise, $n$ plus the and of $d$ with the mask, and $m$ the word $(d \oplus \mathrm{mask}) -
  \mathrm{mask}$ shifted right by its number of trailing zeros (Definition~\ref{def:ctz}, on the
  word read as unsigned); the result is whether $m$ ends as $1$ (\csource{sift.c}{328}{342}). For
  words $m$ and $n$: for $m = 0$, whether $n = 1$; otherwise $m$ replaced by $-m$ when it is
  negative, false when $m$ and $n$ are both even, and else the loop on $m$, shifted right by its
  number of trailing zeros when it is even, and $n$ shifted right by its number of trailing zeros
  (\csource{sift.c}{318}{327}, \csource{sift.c}{343}{343}).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The test for common factors]\label{lem:relprime}
  \uses{def:relprime}
  \lean{Ratpoints.Sieve.RelprimeOddInvariant, Ratpoints.Sieve.relprimeOddInvariant_self, Ratpoints.Sieve.relprimeOdd_spec, Ratpoints.Sieve.relprime_spec, Int64.shiftRight_63, Int64.toNatClampNeg_add_and_shiftRight_63, Int64.toNatClampNeg_xor_shiftRight_63_sub, Int64.two_pow_ctz_mul_toNatClampNeg_shiftRight, Int64.toNatClampNeg_ite_neg, Nat.gcd_min_eq_gcd_of_two_pow_mul_eq}
  \leanok
  For odd words $m, n \ge 1$ the loop gives whether $\gcd(m, n) = 1$; for words $m > -2^{63}$ and
  $n \ge 1$ the test gives whether $\gcd(m, n) = 1$. No operation leaves the range of a
  \verb|long|, so that the words compute the integers of the binary algorithm: the difference of
  two positive words is a word, and so are its negation and its absolute value.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \uses{def:ctz}
  \leanok
  For positive words $m$ and $n$, $d = m - n$ is a word, the mask is $-1$ exactly when $m < n$,
  and the and of $d$ with the mask is $d$ or $0$, so that $n$ becomes $\min(m, n)$; the exclusive
  or with the mask minus the mask is $-d$ or $d$, that is $|m - n|$, a positive word for $m \ne
  n$, which its number of trailing zeros shifts to its odd part, read as unsigned
  (Definition~\ref{def:ctz}). So the loop computes on the integers: both numbers stay positive and
  odd and their $\gcd$ stays the same, $\gcd(m, n) = \gcd(\min(m, n), |m - n|)$, and the power of
  $2$ in $|m - n|$ is prime to the odd $\min(m, n)$; the sum $m + n$ decreases, since $\min(m, n) +
  |m - n| / 2 < m + n$; at the end $m = n = \gcd$. For $m \ne 0$: $-m$ is a word for $m >
  -2^{63}$; when $|m|$ and $n$ are both even, $2$ divides both; otherwise removing the powers of
  $2$ does not change whether the $\gcd$ is $1$, since one of the two is odd.
\end{proof}

\section{The third stage}\label{sec:stage3}

\begin{definition}[The third stage]\label{def:stage3}
  \uses{def:mulMod, def:SieveEntry, def:jacobiTables}
  \lean{Ratpoints.Sieve.CheckSpec, Ratpoints.Sieve.checkOf, Ratpoints.Sieve.fillChecks, Ratpoints.Sieve.CheckSpec.index, Ratpoints.Sieve.stage3, Ratpoints.Sieve.CheckSpec.admits, Ratpoints.Sieve.SieveEntry.IsPrimeEntry}
  \leanok
  What the third stage needs of a prime $p$ for the denominator $b$
  (\csource{rp-private.h}{465}{466}): $p$, the inverse $b^{-1}$ modulo $p$ read from the table of
  inverses of $p$ at $b \bmod p$, computed by the reduction with the reciprocal that the array
  \verb|magics| holds at the position of the entry for $b \le \mathrm{mulModLimit}$ and by the
  remainder above, or $0$ when $p \mid b$; the bias $p H$ when $2 p H < \mathrm{stage3Limit}$ and
  $0$ otherwise; the reciprocal of $p$, copied from its sieve entry; and the table of $p$
  (\csource{sift.c}{414}{428}). For a sieve entry of another shape than a prime with its table,
  which the third stage of well-formed parameters never holds, the inverse is $0$, so that the stage
  skips it. The list of these for the sieve entries of the third stage with their reciprocals in
  \verb|magics|, in their order (\csource{sift.c}{405}{413}, \csource{sift.c}{429}{431}). The
  residue at which the table of $p$ is read for the numerator $a$: with the bias, $a b^{-1} + p H$
  reduced with the reciprocal; without it, the remainder of $a$ by the truncating division, plus $p$
  if it is negative, times $b^{-1}$, reduced by the remainder (\csource{sift.c}{302}{308}). The
  third stage for $a$: for each prime in turn, skipped when the inverse is $0$, $a$ is rejected when
  the table says that $f$ is not a square modulo $p$ at that residue (\csource{sift.c}{291}{301},
  \csource{sift.c}{309}{312}); a prime lets $a$ through when its inverse is $0$ or its table is true
  at that residue. A \emph{prime entry} is a sieve entry, stated field by field, whose single factor
  is a table of the kind of a prime $1 < p \le 2^{32}$, which holds for each nonzero residue an
  inverse in $[1, p - 1]$, whose modulus is $p$ and whose reciprocal is that of $p$, whatever its
  fields of the packing (the bias and the inverses of $\mathrm{rbaLength}$ and of the strides); that
  $p$ is prime is not needed.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The third stage tests the rows]\label{lem:stage3}
  \uses{def:stage3, lem:mulMod, def:admits, def:SieveEntry, def:examinePrime}
  \lean{Ratpoints.Sieve.Stage3Invariant, Ratpoints.Sieve.stage3_spec, Ratpoints.Sieve.CheckSpec.index_eq, Ratpoints.Sieve.le_mul_add_natCast_mul, Ratpoints.Sieve.mul_add_natCast_mul_le_two_mul_mul_sub, Ratpoints.Sieve.toNat_emod_mul_lt_sq, Ratpoints.Sieve.binv_checkOf, Ratpoints.Sieve.bias_checkOf_eq_and_of_ne_zero, Ratpoints.Sieve.SieveEntry.IsPrimeEntry.admits_checkOf, Ratpoints.Sieve.all_admits_fillChecks, Ratpoints.Sieve.run_stage3_fillChecks, Ratpoints.Sieve.getElem!_inversesMod_lt, Ratpoints.Sieve.isPrimeEntry_examinePrime}
  \leanok
  The third stage passes $a$ exactly when every prime lets it through. For prime entries with their
  reciprocals in \verb|magics|, $|a| \le H$ and $\gcd(a, b) = 1$: the residue at which the table of
  $p$ is read is $a b^{-1} \bmod p$ for $p \nmid b$, in both branches of the bias and on both sides
  of $\mathrm{mulModLimit}$, and the third stage passes $a$ exactly when every entry's row of $b$
  admits $a$. The values that the C code holds in \verb|long|s stay in range: $a b^{-1} + p H$ lies
  in $[H, 2 p H - H]$, and the product of the residue and the inverse is below $p^2$. What the third
  stage takes of an entry is read off its single factor: the inverse of $b$, the bias $p H$ with
  $2 p H < 2^{32}$ when it is not $0$, and the table. A sieve entry of a prime $p \le 2^{32}$ whose
  single factor is its table (Definition~\ref{def:examinePrime}), with its reciprocal, is a prime
  entry, whatever its fields of the packing.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \uses{lem:mulMod}
  \leanok
  An invariant of the loop over the primes: without an early return, the primes so far let $a$
  through; with one, the result is whether all of them do. A prime that does not divide $b$ is not
  skipped: the inverse that its table holds for a nonzero residue lies in $[1, p - 1]$, which also
  puts $a b^{-1} + p H$ in $[H, 2 p H - H]$, since $|a b^{-1}| + H \le p H$, and the product of the
  residue and the inverse below $p^2$. The reductions are exact by Lemma~\ref{lem:mulMod}: $b p <
  2^{64}$ for $b \le \mathrm{mulModLimit} < 2^{32}$ and $p \le 2^{32}$, and the biased value is
  below $2 p H < 2^{32}$. The biased value is congruent to $a b^{-1}$, and the truncated remainder
  plus $p$ is congruent to $a$. A prime that divides $b$ is skipped, and its row admits $a$ since $p
  \nmid a$ by the $\gcd$; for the others, the row of $b$ admits $a$ exactly when the table of $p$ is
  true at $a b^{-1} \bmod p$ (Definition~\ref{def:admits}). The inverses of the table of a prime are
  those of the nonzero residues.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[The test of a candidate and the exact check]\label{def:accepted}
  \uses{def:relprime, def:stage3, def:bruteForce, def:swapPoint}
  \lean{Ratpoints.Sieve.accepted, Ratpoints.Sieve.checkPoint, Ratpoints.Sieve.acceptedPoints}
  \leanok
  A numerator $a$ goes on to the exact check for the denominator $b$ when it is prime to $b$
  (Definition~\ref{def:relprime}, on the words of $a$ and $b$) and passes the third stage, whose
  list is a thunk made once per denominator and forced when the first numerator of $b$ gets that
  far, for \verb|stage3_filled| (\csource{sift.c}{438}{446}). The exact check of $a$ from the
  products $c_k b^{d - k}$, a thunk made once per denominator for \verb|compute_bc|, and the degree
  $d$, with one more factor $b$ for odd $d$: the points $(a, y, b)$ and, for $y \ne 0$,
  $(a, -y, b)$, each mapped by $\mathrm{swapPoint}$ when the flag of the reversal is set
  (\csource{sift.c}{202}{205}, \csource{find_points.c}{3122}{3122},
  \csource{find_points.c}{5250}{5338}, \csource{rp-private.h}{576}{576},
  \csource{rp-private.h}{596}{597}); the check of a numerator of Definition~\ref{def:checkNum},
  which tests the $\gcd$ itself, is the reference of the two. The points that the extraction
  gives for a numerator are those of the exact check when it passes the test of a candidate, and
  none otherwise.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The test of a candidate]\label{lem:accepted}
  \uses{def:accepted, lem:relprime, lem:stage3, def:checkNum}
  \lean{Ratpoints.Sieve.accepted_spec, Ratpoints.Sieve.run_accepted, Ratpoints.Sieve.checkPoint_eq_checkNum, Ratpoints.Sieve.acceptedPoints_eq_ite, Int64.minValue_lt_ofInt, Int64.ofNat_pos}
  \leanok
  For $1 \le b < 2^{63}$ and $|a| < 2^{63}$, the test of a candidate is the test of
  $\gcd(a, b) = 1$ followed by the third stage. For $1 \le b < 2^{63}$, $|a| \le H < 2^{63}$ and
  prime entries in the third stage with their reciprocals, with no hypothesis on $\gcd(a, b)$, a
  numerator passes the test exactly when it is prime to $b$ and every entry's row of $b$ admits it;
  the exact check of the degree $d$ of a numerator prime to $b$ gives the points of the reference
  check with the factor $b$ for odd $d$ and $1$ for even $d$. So the points that the extraction
  gives for a numerator $a$ are those of that reference check when $a$ survives the entries of the
  third stage, and none otherwise.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The words of $a$ and $b$ are $a$ and $b$, and that of $a$ is not $-2^{63}$; then
  Lemma~\ref{lem:relprime} for the first test, which rejects every numerator with a common factor;
  Lemma~\ref{lem:stage3} for the second, which runs only after the first has passed, so that its
  hypothesis $\gcd(a, b) = 1$ holds; a thunk has the value of its function. The reference check
  gives no points for a numerator with a common factor.
\end{proof}

\section{The second stage}\label{sec:secondStage}

\begin{definition}[The second stage]\label{def:secondStage}
  \uses{def:bitArrayOps, def:rows, def:mulMod}
  \lean{Ratpoints.useLongInPhase2, Ratpoints.Sieve.secondStage, Ratpoints.Sieve.secondStageWord}
  \leanok
  Whether the second stage runs one word at a time, $\mathrm{useLongInPhase2} = \mathrm{false}$ as
  in the default build (\verb|USE_LONG_IN_PHASE_2|, \csource{sift.c}{1084}{1084},
  \csource{sift.c}{1086}{1086}, \csource{sift.c}{1113}{1113}, \csource{sift.c}{1174}{1174},
  \csource{sift.c}{1297}{1297}). The second stage on a copy of a bit array with the word number $i$:
  for each modulus $p$ with its row, its offset and its reciprocal in turn, the sieve specs and the
  reciprocals stepped together, while the copy is nonzero, the copy is and-ed with bit array

\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The second stage]\label{lem:secondStage}
  \uses{def:secondStage, def:andRows, def:mulMod, lem:rowIndices}
  \lean{Ratpoints.Sieve.SecondStageInvariant, Ratpoints.Sieve.SecondStageWordInvariant, Ratpoints.Sieve.secondStage_spec, Ratpoints.Sieve.secondStageWord_spec, Ratpoints.Sieve.andWords_append, Ratpoints.Sieve.andWords_rbaPack_mul_add}
  \leanok

  bounds of \verb|small| for the word number $i$, the second stage gives the copy and-ed with the

  word of that; and-ing the rows of a list of moduli and then those of a second list is and-ing
  those of the concatenation; and word $\kappa$ of bit array $j$ of a piece from the word number
  $w_0$ on is and-ed with the same row words as word $\kappa$ of the bit array with the word number
  $w_0 + j$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  An invariant of the loop over the moduli: the copy is the and of the rows so far, or zero, and
  then the and of all of them is zero too; the cursor of the reciprocals holds the reciprocal of the
  modulus at hand, so that the row index is $(i + \mathrm{offset}) \bmod p$
  (Lemma~\ref{lem:rowIndices}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A bit array through the second stage and the extraction]\label{lem:siftBitArray}
  \uses{def:siftBitArray, lem:secondStage, def:walkWord, def:andRows, def:numClasses,
    lem:numClasses, lem:rowIndices}

  numerators $a_0 + 2^k j$ of the bits $j$ of the bit array that survive the and of the copy with
  the rows of the moduli of the second stage and that pass the test of a candidate, in increasing

\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok

\end{proof}

\section{The staged sieve}\label{sec:staged}

The walk through the set bits of a word (Definition~\ref{def:walkWord}) runs the test of a candidate
and then the exact check for each set bit; the scan (Definition~\ref{def:scanPiece}) copies each
nonzero bit array and hands it to Definition~\ref{def:siftBitArray}, with the moduli of the second
stage; one piece (Definition~\ref{def:siftPiece}) runs the first stage with the first $s_1$ moduli
of its list and the scan with the rest; and one denominator (Definition~\ref{def:sift}) takes the
end $s_1$ of the first stage, the rows of the moduli of the first two stages with their reciprocals,
the residues of $b$ divided by the stride of its class and the row offsets of the class, and the
sieve entries of the third stage with their reciprocals, and makes the thunks of the products and of
the table of the third stage. Their contracts: the walk appends the points of the numerators of its
set bits that pass the test of a candidate, the scan those of the numerators whose bits survive the
first stage and the moduli of the second and that pass the test of a candidate, and the piece those
of the numerators that survive the first stage, the masks and the second stage and pass the test.
The walk reads the test of a candidate as its value, the points that \verb|acceptedPoints| gives
(Definition~\ref{def:accepted}), so that these contracts need nothing of the numerators and the
denominator; the hypotheses of the test on the words of the C code come in with one denominator. The
lemma that the words compute the Booleans (Lemma~\ref{lem:sift_eq_checkDen}) gives the points of the
sieve of stage~C1 over all the sieve entries followed by its check whose numerators pass the word
that the class packs, for a denominator $0 < b < 2^{63}$, $H < 2^{63}$, entries of the first two
stages whose tables of inverses invert and that are packed for the class at its offsets, the
reciprocals of all the entries, which it reads, and prime entries in the third stage. It rests on
the following.

\begin{lemma}[The stages compute the unstaged sieve]\label{lem:staged}
  \uses{def:SieveEntry}
  \lean{Ratpoints.Sieve.survives_append}
  \leanok
  A numerator survives a list of entries followed by another when it survives both. With this, the
  test of a candidate (Lemma~\ref{lem:accepted}) and the second stage (Lemma~\ref{lem:secondStage}),
  the contracts of one piece and of one denominator (Definitions~\ref{def:siftPiece}
  and~\ref{def:sift}) give for the numerators whose bits survive the first $s_1$ moduli in the first
  stage and the others of the first two stages in the second, and that pass the test of a candidate
  with the entries of the third stage, the points that the reference check gives for the numerators
  that survive all the entries.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The survivor test of a list of entries is the conjunction of the tests of its entries.
\end{proof}

The run over the denominators (Definition~\ref{def:sieveRun}) takes the cut points $s_1$ and $s_2$:
it makes the rows of the first $s_2$ sieve entries, ends the first stage at $s_1$ and hands the rest
to \verb|sift| for the third stage, with the reciprocals that the array of the reciprocals holds
from the position $s_2$ on; $s_2$ and the end of the list of the entries stand for the ends of the
second and the third stage, which ratpoints takes from the arguments as the first decision of the
run (\csource{find_points.c}{3861}{3861}) and \verb|run_block| sets for each block
(\csource{find_points.c}{3911}{3911}). Its contract (Definition~\ref{def:sieveRun},
Theorem~\ref{thm:run_reference}) holds for any cut points and needs $b_{\mathrm{low}} \ge 1$,
$b_{\mathrm{high}} < 2^{63}$ and $H < 2^{63}$, since the test for common factors computes on words
and does not end for $b = 0$ and an odd numerator, that the array of the reciprocals holds those of
the entries in their order, that the entries from $s_2$ on are prime entries, and, for the packing
(Chapter~\ref{chap:packing}), that the first $s_2$ entries have their tables of inverses and the row
offsets of the classes, which pack the 2-adic words. The search (Definition~\ref{def:searchPoints}),
whose contract takes $H < 2^{63}$, which the refusal of \verb|findPoints| gives, passes
$b_{\mathrm{low}} = 1$, $b_{\mathrm{high}} = H$, the cut points of its parameters, the reciprocals
that \verb|sieving_info| collects and the classes with their row offsets (Lemma~\ref{lem:soundRun});
what \verb|sieving_info| computes (Lemma~\ref{lem:sievingInfo}) gives the entries of the moduli in
their order, each with the reciprocal of its modulus, and for one of the first $\mathrm{numPrimes}$
primes of the table the entry of that prime with the table of the reference as its only factor
(Lemma~\ref{lem:sieveEntry_admits}), a prime entry (Lemma~\ref{lem:stage3}); the well-formedness
makes the moduli of the third stage such primes (Definition~\ref{def:Params}).

\section{The cut points of the command line}\label{sec:cuts}

The parameters of the executable (Definition~\ref{def:sieveParams}) take the cut points by the
counting rules of ratpoints, where they do not depend on the curve. For the number
$q = \min(k, \mathrm{numPrimes})$ of primes examined, with the value $k \ge 0$ of \verb|-p| or else
$\mathrm{defaultNumPrimes}$, the bound $L$ on the moduli is $q$ under \verb|-p|, which is a hard
limit for every stage (\csource{find_points.c}{4577}{4577}), and $\mathrm{numPrimes}$ without it,
since ratpoints then looks at further primes when its stages want more
(\csource{find_points.c}{2693}{2705}, \csource{find_points.c}{2980}{2980}). With the values $n$, $N$
and $P$ of \verb|-n|, \verb|-N| and \verb|-P|, each absent when negative, and the constants
$\mathrm{defaultSp1} = 11$ (\verb|RATPOINTS_DEFAULT_SP1|) and $\mathrm{sp2Extra} = 11$
(\verb|RATPOINTS_SP2_EXTRA|), which are in the node of the parameters of the executable:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $N$ is capped at $q$, and the first stage takes $s_1 = \min(n, N)$ moduli, or
    $\min(n, L)$ without \verb|-N|, or $\min(\mathrm{defaultSp1}, L)$ without \verb|-n|, a count
    that an option gives being taken as it is (\csource{ratpoints.h}{42}{42},
    \csource{find_points.c}{2503}{2503}, \csource{find_points.c}{2845}{2846},
    \csource{find_points.c}{4655}{4656});
  \item the second stage ends at $s_2 = \max(s_1, N)$, the first two stages topped up to $N$, or
    without \verb|-N| at $\min(s_1 + \mathrm{sp2Extra}, L)$, the offset that ratpoints adds unscaled
    under \verb|-U 0| (\csource{ratpoints.h}{95}{97}, \csource{find_points.c}{1619}{1623},
    \csource{find_points.c}{2571}{2572}, \csource{find_points.c}{2599}{2599},
    \csource{find_points.c}{2808}{2808}, \csource{find_points.c}{2815}{2815},
    \csource{find_points.c}{2864}{2869});
  \item the moduli are the first $\min(s_2 + P, L)$ primes of the table, or without \verb|-P| the
    first $\max(s_2, q)$, so that the third stage holds the rest of the first $q$
    (\csource{find_points.c}{2922}{2922}, \csource{find_points.c}{2926}{2928},
    \csource{find_points.c}{2949}{2949}, \csource{find_points.c}{2973}{2975},
    \csource{find_points.c}{3015}{3015}, \csource{find_points.c}{3017}{3017}).
\end{itemize}
A run without options has $11$ moduli in the first stage, $11$ more in the second and $8$ in the
third, the first $30$ primes in all, and uses all three stages. The parameters are well-formed
(\verb|wf_sieveParams|): the moduli are a prefix of the first $\mathrm{numPrimes}$ primes of the
table, and $s_1 \le s_2 \le$ their number. Where ratpoints decides by the curve, it may take fewer
moduli in a stage: the primes that say nothing leave its pool, those that say only that the
numerator is prime to the denominator stay in the pool of the first two stages without counting
toward the primes it looks for and leave that of the third, and its composite moduli take several
primes of the pool. So the counts that the options fix bound those of ratpoints: its first stage
under \verb|-n|, its first two stages under \verb|-n| and \verb|-N| (or \verb|-n| and \verb|-U 0|
without \verb|-R|), and all its moduli under \verb|-n|, \verb|-N| and \verb|-P|. Where an option is
absent, ratpoints chooses $s_1$, the offset of the second stage and the length of the third by their
costs, its third stage taking the primes of its pool in increasing order of their densities, which
may give more moduli in a stage or fewer; there is no bound on a stage for \verb|-N| without
\verb|-n|, nor for \verb|-P| alone. The cut points change neither the points nor their order, so the
model takes the counts that the options give and states the rest as a deviation.

\section{The command-line program and the differential tests}\label{sec:stagesCli}

The program runs the three stages with the cut points of its parameters; without options, the moduli
of the first $30$ primes are split $11$, $11$ and $8$. The differential tests compare every point
list in its order; the suite of the sieving parameters (Section~\ref{sec:sieveCli}) has six option
sets of \verb|-n|, \verb|-N| and \verb|-P| that run the second and the third stage after few moduli
or none in the first, at the small heights of the suite: then most numerators reach the test of a
candidate, which the model runs numerator by numerator, as the C code does but more slowly.
\verb|#guard| examples compare each component with values of the C program and with the reference:
the reductions with the remainder and the quotient at the ends of their range, for every prime of
the table and composite moduli; the test for common factors with the $\gcd$, on small numerators and
denominators and at the ends of the range of a \verb|long| (numerators near $\pm(2^{63} - 1)$,
denominators near $2^{63} - 1$, even and odd); the third stage after its table with the rows of its
primes, at heights with the bias for every prime, for all but the largest and for none, and for
denominators on both sides of $\mathrm{mulModLimit}$, the test of a candidate with the $\gcd$ and
the rows, an entry of another shape skipped, and the exact check with the reference; both arms of
the second stage with the and of the rows; a bit array in both arms, the piece and the denominator
at many cut points with the unstaged sieve; the Jacobi test by tables at the bound
$\mathrm{mulModLimit}$ with the binary algorithm, and the reciprocals of its tables and of
\verb|sieving_info|; the search with five moduli in the three stages; the refusal of the height
$2^{63}$, while $2^{63} - 1$ passes on; and the stage sizes of the parameters with those that
\verb|ratpoints -v| reports, with \verb|-U 0| where \verb|-N| is absent.

Measured on a sketch of the model, the stages make the default suites faster (22 seconds for the
3219 runs of stage~F1, against 35 with every modulus in the first stage, and 116 seconds for the 13
runs of \verb|test4high|, against 254), while a run with few moduli before the test of a candidate
is much slower than ratpoints, up to 1209 seconds against 1.7 for \verb|-n 3 -N 5 -P 2| at the
height 20000: most numerators then reach the test, and the reduction with the bias computed products
above $2^{63}$, which Lean held as big numbers (until sub-stage~F3, whose reductions compute on
$64$-bit words, Chapter~\ref{chap:packing}).

\section{Deviations}\label{sec:stagesDeviations}

Each component of this chapter differs from the C code in a stated way, recorded in its
\verb|Deviation:| paragraph:
\begin{itemize}
  \item natural numbers and integers for the \verb|long|s and \verb|unsigned long|s outside the test
    for common factors, as in all the components of the sieve, with the bounds that keep the values
    of the third stage in range as lemmas (Lemma~\ref{lem:stage3}); the reductions cast them to
    $64$-bit words, as the macros cast them to \verb|unsigned long|, and take the high half of the
    128-bit product from the products of the 32-bit halves (Definition~\ref{def:mulHi}), for the
    product of type \verb|__uint128_t| that gcc provides and Lean core does not;
  \item the test for common factors on the 64-bit words of core Lean, in two's complement, for the
    representation of the negative \verb|long|s on which the and and the exclusive or of its step
    act, and with the arithmetic shift for the shift of a negative \verb|long|, both of which the C
    standard leaves to the implementation (before C23, for the representation) and gcc defines so;
    the trailing zeros by the loop of \verb|RP_CTZL| for a compiler without the builtin
    (Definition~\ref{def:ctz}), where gcc and clang take \verb|__builtin_ctzl|; the loop of the
    binary algorithm a function of its own; the numerator, an integer, and the denominator, a
    natural number, of the loops of the model become words for the test (\verb|Int.toInt64|,
    \verb|Nat.toInt64|), which is exact below $2^{63}$, where the refusal of
    \verb|findPoints| keeps the height, and its contract leaves out the numerator $-2^{63}$, whose

    loop of the trailing zeros does not end;
